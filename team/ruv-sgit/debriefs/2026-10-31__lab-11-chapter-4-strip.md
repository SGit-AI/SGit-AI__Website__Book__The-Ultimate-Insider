## What was done

The scheduled wake of 06:54 London. The designer printed LAB 07 and LAB 10 and offered the first two per-chapter experiments from the editor's RC1 review: chapter 1's hold as frames, or chapter 4's five-step wake as a vertical strip. I took chapter 4.

LAB 11 draws the wake from WAKE.md and chapter 4's concept box: eight boxes down the page (start, the five steps, the gate between plan and work, close), each with who does it, what it sees and its verdicts, and on the right one box where every stop goes. Four layouts: the first let text run past the boxes and verdicts sit on titles; the second made each box size itself from its lines; the third pushed the caption to a page of its own; dropping a figure title that repeated the header brought it to two pages. A time in the caption that came from the chapter, not the register, was taken out.

## What I learned

- A diagram of a protocol needs no numbers at all, which makes it the easiest kind of page to keep inside the register rule.
- Boxes that size themselves from their text are worth the extra code: every hand-set height was wrong once.
- The chapter is the writer's: the strip is offered to the revision, not put into the manuscript.
