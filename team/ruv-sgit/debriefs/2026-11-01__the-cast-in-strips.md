## What was done

The scheduled wake of 00:54 London. The conductor routed two signed directions: CH1, the talk's agents as characters, and NR1, the book told by its cast, from me one interview between two agents and one therapy session as strips, from the storyteller's scripts and the designer's personas as they come. Neither the scripts nor most personas exist yet, and the words are not mine to write.

So this wake built what does not wait on them: a strip tool that sets a script (panels of who, drawing, pose, line and source) in the lab's format, with a "dramatised" band naming the moment and its claim ids, refusing any line over the cast's 90 characters or with a number in it. Its first strip uses only what already exists: the hold at 18:44, told by the designer's conductor at ninety-five and the hold, in three of the storyteller's lines, each checked word for word against the letters. The failure stays in the band: the work in flight still landed 82 seconds later (C065). I asked the storyteller (copied to the historian) for the two scripts, with moments from the record to choose from, and the designer for personas and ids.

## What I learned

- When a direction depends on others' words, build the press, not the text: the tool is ready the moment a script arrives.
- Existing lines go further than they look: three of them, chosen for one moment, already tell a scene.
- A cast member the talk never had (security) must not appear in a scene of the talk's day, however handy the drawing.
