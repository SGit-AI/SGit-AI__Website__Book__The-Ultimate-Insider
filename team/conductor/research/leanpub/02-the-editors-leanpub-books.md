## 4. What the pattern says

What the pages show:
- Nothing is at 100%. The percentages are 60, 60, 60, 64, 80, 80, 85, 95, 95, and Software Quality shows none. Four books are at 60 or 64, three at 80 to 85, two at 95.
- The last update to any book is 2018-10-08. Updates fall in four clusters: March to April 2014 (three books), January to February 2016 (four), December 2016 (two), October 2018 (one). Within the visible record that is an update burst per book and then nothing shown since; I have no per-book revision history, so I cannot give a cadence beyond these last dates.
- The two 95% books are among the shortest by words (Jni4Net 5,293, Hacking Portugal 14,487), which suggests the nearest to finished were small and tightly scoped. That is my reading of the numbers, not his statement.
- Best by public signal (readers): Practical AngularJS 8,001, Practical Git and GitHub 2,676, then SecDevOps 803. The next five sit between 230 and 465. The two most-held books are practical, how-to books made from blog posts, at 80 to 85% complete, with the lowest suggested price ($7.99) and a free option. The conceptual and opinion books (Thoughts on OWASP 238, Generation Z 465) have fewer readers. These are holders including free copies, not paying buyers, and I cannot say revenue.
- What the "Practical" books had going for them, on the page: a concrete subject, a bundle at $10 against $37.98 separate, a free package, and (AngularJS) a printed-copy package.
- Where they stalled: his statement is that he was still discovering each book while writing it. The pages are consistent with that for the later, conceptual books (SecDevOps, Generation Z, Hacking Portugal all point to a repository of ideas and issues, and ask for feedback as issues), but a page cannot confirm a reason. I add no other reason.

## 5. What the new way of making a book changes

Only what follows from his statement and from these pages: the old books show a percentage below 100 on their front, and a visible "in progress" marker is part of how Leanpub presents them. This book starts as a complete first version made by a team of agents and is refined afterwards, so the discovering he describes happened before the first version, not through it. What the record cannot say is how a refined complete book will read or sell.

## What this means for the book

- Present it as complete and being refined. Leanpub's own "N% complete" line is how his older pages read as unfinished; if the book is finished, set it to the finished state rather than leaving a percentage.
- Price against his own catalogue: every older book was "Free With Membership" with a $7.99 to $11.00 suggested price and a free minimum, and the best-held books sat at $7.99. A single bundle at $10 beat the sum of $37.98. Whether to keep a free minimum is the editor's decision; the public record only shows the free option on all of them.
- Write a real blurb and fill every field. Two of his older pages have empty About sections and none show reviews or testimonials; this book should say who made it, how, and what it is for.
- Offer an optional paid package beyond the plain ebook (AngularJS had a printed-copy package; Git and AngularJS had a $15 tier), if a print edition exists.
- Put the text and issues in a public repository if the editor wants feedback the way SecDevOps and Hacking Portugal did, and keep the licence stated on the page.
- Show a last-updated date that is recent. Every older book's last update is 2018 or earlier, which is what a visitor sees first.
- Open items: cover images, any Testimonials tab content, and reader totals for Hacking Portugal, O2 Platform and Software Quality were not shown; his blog posts about the books were not read.
