# The editor's guidance for the full book: one checklist for every chapter

Kept by conductor@book.the-ultimate-insider, 4 November 2026, from the editor's notes in `briefs/2026-11-04__in-session/01` to `21` (his words are there, verbatim; this is the conductor's summary, for applying them to every chapter, not only the pages he saw). Decisions cited are in book.json. Full book only (FB1); aim: Thursday 5 November, 10:00 London (TG2, a target, not a freeze; it was Wednesday 20:00, TG1).

## The voice
1. **The narrator is him, speaking now**, with opinion and commentary: what it meant, what surprised him, where it came from (07, 13, 17). Not a reporter of the record.
2. **The agents tell what they did**, in their own voice ("and then I did this"); the narrator comments (17). Fewer narrator turns (15).
3. **No logistics in the reading text**: no recording-clock stamps, "added after the day", times to the second, "twenty-four minutes after" (07, 15). Times only when the time is the story.
4. **No quoting him to justify the text**; no long quotes of his own voice; "as I said in the talk" at most (04, 07).
5. **Never judge the reader** ("easy to miss", "as you know"); "Do this now" is an invitation, not an order (12; the newsroom's rule 11).
6. **No repeated content**: where a passage and a figure say the same thing, keep one (03, 17).
7. The newsroom's style sheet is the narrator's (`team/conductor/from-newsroom/2026-11-04__narrator-voice-part-one.md`, section 2), in the storyteller's cast guide.

## The cast
8. **Each agent introduced at its first appearance**, early, the conductor too: a card or side panel in its own voice (role, areas, what it did, where it added value, who it worked with) with registered stats (13).
9. **No mood tags** in speaker lines ("happy, the fast one") (13; the storyteller rules on stage directions).
10. **Cast bars drawn**, not printed as text (05); greyed cards show the real drawings (12).

## The structure
11. **Start here** as a creative overview and index of the book (03); **an introduction** before chapter 1: him as narrator, the cast, why the book is told this way, the journey together, feedback, the companion site (04).
12. **Each hour chapter opens with that hour's character** (setup, crunch, flow, acceleration, slowdown; the hours he was absent told as such); **a day at a glance** before the hours (15).
13. **Important-concept callouts**, a sibling of the words boxes, where each concept first comes up: the team's folder as memory, the repository growing, the publishing flow, a JSON file as a GET endpoint; his past projects (newsroom.sgit.ai and the other sgit.ai sites) as memory (18, 19).
14. **The weight of the documents**: their size and a few pages, in a collage (19).
15. **The admin pages** in the book, the keys page and its QR codes among them, each image cleared by security (AD1, 14).
16. **"Words for this page"** after the words' first use (13); **"Do this now"** as a visual card with its objective (10, 12); **no "Where this came from" in print** (WC1, 10).
17. **Why the team stopped**: time ran out, diminishing returns; the 95% a coincidence, the hold file quoted as it was (C089, 13, 20).
