## 1. The verdict, on one page

The cast works. The nine are distinct, the drawings carry them, and the best scenes are good enough to quote in a review: the conductor caught by the reviewer it invented (p40), the researcher arguing with its own checker by pointing at its rules (p48), studio refusing a relayed word (p80), ruv-sgit at an empty bench (p56), deck "rearranging slides that will not be shown" (p88), the designer's "Ah. The hold." (p83). Those scenes do what the book promised in "Who is telling this story": the agents tell the day as it happened, and fail on the page the way the record says they did.

Five things stop it from being ready for wider review:

1. **Agents recite the record instead of speaking.** Too many lines are the record read aloud in the first person: file names, chapter numbers, counts and sequences of events. The worst cases are the high five, which is a list of numbers (p84); the conductor's opening of hour nineteen, which narrates five other members' actions (p82); the handover walk-through, four long conductor paragraphs (p103 to 104); and the exit interviews (p34 to 37), which are summaries, not interviews.
2. **The cast guide's own rule 7 is broken.** "No count, time, percentage or other figure in an agent's line." The high five has seven numbers in four bubbles. The tally card beside it already shows them.
3. **The narrator says it twice.** In about a dozen scenes the narrator states the point, the agent restates it, and sometimes the narrator explains the agent's line afterwards. Either the agent's line or the narrator's sentence should go.
4. **"Dramatised." appears 95 times.** It now sits after narrator-only passages, after concept boxes, after figures, and as an orphan at the top of pages. At this density it is noise, and it marks things that are not dramatised.
5. **Formulas and repeats.** "Hey, I'm X, and this is where I first appear" opens 21 introductions; "(on the minute)" tags the conductor seven times; several lines appear twice in the book (the researcher's "a claim without a captured source is a gap", security's "I read what the others write, and I report. I do not fix.", studio's relayed word, told three times).

None of this needs new scenes. It needs cuts, and about thirty lines rewritten. The priorities are in section 6.

## 3. Scenes

### Scenes that work, and should not be touched

- **The first wake caught by its own reviewer** (p39 to 41). Once stage one is cut down (section 2), this is the book's best scene.
- **The argument with a reviewer** (p48), with ruv-sgit's two-panel card.
- **The objection kept** (p74). The storyteller "to itself" is the right touch.
- **An empty queue** (p56).
- **A relayed word refused** (p80).
- **The hold** (p83), up to and including "Ah. The hold."
- **What did not work** (p87 to 91). Every card has its owner speaking, the owner owns the miss, and ruv-sgit's strip (the researcher interviewing deck) is a small gem.
- **The historian's own error** (p108).

### Scenes to cut, merge or rewrite

1. **The high five (p84).** The narrator says it is imagined ("this is how it might have gone"), which is honest. But it is four lines of numbers, the tally card beside it shows the same numbers, and it breaks the cast guide's rule 7. Rewrite it as feeling, not tally:
   > **Proposed:**
   > Conductor: Look at what we did. One day.
   > ruv-sgit (looking up, for once): The bench is empty. Everything I made went in.
   > Deck: Part one: every slide reads in five seconds.
   > Dev: And every fix he asked for came back to him.
   > Conductor: Not any one of us. The same folders, the same rules.
   >
   > The tally card stays, with the numbers.

   *Check:* ruv-sgit's "everything I made went in" is too strong if some pieces were not placed before the hold (the afternoon-asks card says some showcases were not). Use "Everything I made is waiting on the page" if so.

2. **The exit interviews (p34 to 37).** Eight paragraphs of a hundred to a hundred and fifty words each, "shortened from the historian's reconstruction". They read as debrief summaries, not interviews, and the label (which is long, and bold) tells the reader the words are nobody's. Two options:
   - **(a)** Keep them, as two short answers each: "What went well?" and "What did not?", two or three sentences each, in the agent's own register (dev quick, the conductor procedural, deck exacting). The full versions go online, with the augmented edition.
   - **(b)** Cut them from print, since part four tells every miss at full size with the owner speaking, and keep only the one-line openers (p34 "My last session is the lesson my record keeps", p35 "I ran the clock, and my interview is the hardest on me", "I turned a freeze into a programme", "My workshop answered deck's programme one session at a time"). They are good lines.

   My suggestion is (a). Either way, fix the inconsistency: dev, the conductor, deck, ruv-sgit and the researcher open with their own line; the designer, the storyteller, motion and studio open with a **Narrator** line. Give the last four their own opener too.

   And one passage is broken: at the end of studio's interview (p37), "Part three has why: late in the afternoon, studio's session would not take my word when another agent relayed it to studio, and asked me directly instead." It has no speaker label. It reads as studio talking about itself in the third person, with "my word" meaning Dinis's. Label it **Narrator.**, or cut it; p80 tells it.

3. **The handover walked through (p103 to 104).** See the conductor's section. Four paragraphs become two lines and a figure.

4. **Three letters in a row (p59 to 60).** One quoted note, one deck line, one researcher line. See deck.

5. **The book team's roll call (p105 to 106).** Four lines and the drawing. See section 2.

6. **The cast introductions (21 of them).** The convention (brief 15) is that each agent introduces itself when it arrives. Keep it, but the formula "Hey, I'm X, and this is where I first appear" opens nine of them, and most then read out the agent's scope ("I may change the build, the check, the release pipeline, the site's own pages and the reality file"). That is the policy file, not the character. Keep the scope in the small data panel (it is already there as "On stage from…"), and let the line carry the temperament:
   > **Dev, now:** Hey, I'm dev, and this is where I first appear. I do the build, the gate and the release. I may change the build, the check, the release pipeline, the site's own pages and the reality file. Not the talk's slides; those are deck's. The build every change goes through is mine.
   >
   > **Dev, proposed:** Hey, I'm dev. First in, before the conductor. The build, the gate, the release: if it goes out, it went through me. The slides are deck's. Don't let me near them.
   >
   > **Deck, proposed:** Hey, I'm deck. The talk is mine: what it says, and what it shows. A slide only seen as code is a slide not seen, and I will send yours back.
   >
   > **The researcher, proposed:** Hey, I'm the researcher. Give me a slot and a folder. Everything else, I will check, including you.

   *Check:* "including you" is in character (the only one who tells the editor no, in writing), and the register shows it did.

### The "Dramatised." mark

It appears 95 times in the 144 pages. The convention is signed (NR1), and the cast guide asks for "the lightest form". At this density it is not light: it appears after narrator-only passages (p30 after the publishing-flow concept box; p53, p55 and p56 after paragraphs with no agent line), after every one of the thirteen tour stops (p116 to 121), and orphaned at the top of a page, away from the scene it belongs to (p38, p43, p84, p91, p98).

**Proposed:** one mark per scene, attached to the scene's first agent line, as a small grey tag beside the speaker head; none after narrator-only passages or concept boxes; and in the tour, where every stop is dramatised, one mark for the whole tour in its opening "How to read the scenes" note and none per stop. That honours NR1 and halves the noise. This is a layout change for the publisher, not a text change.

### Moods and stage directions

The mood labels are gone, as brief 13 asked. What remains is mostly good: "(already typing)", "(to ruv-sgit)", "(writing)", "(to itself, rearranging slides that will not be shown)", "(ticking a list)", "(reading its own handover)". Remove the costume descriptions in the tour (motion's cap and clapperboard, studio's headphones, p119) and cut "(on the minute)" down from seven to two.

On the page, the speaker heads are a good touch and should stay. One drawing detail: on the tally card (p84), deck's and the researcher's faces are plain boxes, without the bow tie and the round glasses they have elsewhere, so the two look alike. Worth a note to the designer.

## 4. The hand-off between narrator and agents

The rule in the narrator's style sheet is "hand over cleanly: set it up in a sentence or two and get out of the way". The most common fault is the narrator saying the point, the agent restating it, and, in a few places, the narrator explaining the agent's line afterwards. Each pair below says the same thing twice; in each, keep one.

| Page | The narrator says | The agent then says | Keep |
|---|---|---|---|
| p29 to 30 | the conductor pulled its reach back within three minutes, by changing a rule | "I made their tasks for them. That was a reach too far." | the agent's line; shorten the narrator to the fact |
| p30 | the check stopped dev's own release until the lines were added | "The check said no. It was right. The lines went in, and it said yes." | the agent's line; cut the narrator's sentence |
| p41 | (after dev's line) "That last line is the pre-review's, made into dev's habit…" | "It changes the gate for everyone. I said so in the commit, and I ran the whole gate before I pushed." | the agent's line; the narrator's explanation can go |
| p45 | "That is the lesson the register draws: redaction does not reach a commit already pushed." | "A review that runs after the push is a review that runs late." | the agent's line |
| p46 to 47 | "The designer's scope is the smallest on the team…" | "One folder, mine…" (after its card said the same) | one of the three |
| p88 | the lock was built and never run, because the one member allowed was busy | two dev lines saying the same | the merged dev line in section 2 |
| p88 | the storyteller's turn came between deadlines | "My turn came between deadlines, and I missed the moment" | the second storyteller line only |
| p93 | the scope line, quoted and explained | studio's "So it went on a slide" | both; they say different things |

The better pattern, already used at p48, p56 and p74: the narrator gives the situation in a sentence, the agent speaks, and the narrator says only what the agent cannot know (what it meant later, what Dinis thinks of it).
