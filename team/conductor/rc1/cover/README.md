# Cover concepts for RC1

Three concepts for the cover of The Ultimate Insider (working title), each a 6 by 9 inch page in `cover-N.html` (inline CSS and SVG, system fonts only), rendered to `cover-N.jpg` at 1800 by 2700 pixels and to `cover-N-thumb.jpg` at 600 by 900. The "Release candidate 1" corner tag is the single `.tag` element in each file: delete it for the edition. The subtitle on all three is a placeholder from the RC's own framing: "A team of AI agents built a security talk in a day. The team itself was the insider."

**Concept 1, Open from the inside.** A ring of nine small agent nodes, joined edge to edge, encircles a doorway lit from within; the door has swung outward towards the reader, and a small figure stands in the light. The idea is the book's turn in chapter 9: the threat model looks outward at the locked door, but the insider is already in the room, with the access to open it, and here that insider is the team itself, held together by the ring of its own rules. The ring is the mandate and the boundary; the open door is the reach the chapter measures. Nothing is broken: the door was opened from the inside, which is the benign version of the argument the book insists on. At a glance a print reader sees a warm door of light on deep navy under a big two-line title; at arm's length it is a bright rectangle in a circle, which survives as a thumbnail. The figure doubles as a lower-case "i", which can be read as "insider" or left alone.

**Concept 2, The day on one dial.** A twenty-four-hour clock face with midnight at the top. From 00:16 the rim fills with thin strokes, sparse in the small hours and dense by evening, until a red hand stops at 19:00; the rest of the dial, the evening the team did not get, is a dotted line. It fits the book's spine: the whole story is one day, told from a record timestamped to the second, and the stop at 19:00 is the stage, with the hold just before it. The strokes are illustrative, not plotted from the record (every number in the book must come from the claims register; the researcher could supply the real commit times if the editor picks this one, and then the dial would be data). At a glance it is a typographic, editorial cover on warm paper; at arm's length it reads as a clock nearly full, which says "a day" before the title does. It is the least direct about the insider itself.

**Concept 3, The badge.** A staff pass on a lanyard: where the photograph should be is a small network of nine agents, the word INSIDER beneath it, and a footer stripe reading "Mandate, Boundaries". An insider is, in the plainest image anyone has, a person with a pass; the twist is that this pass belongs to a team, not a face, which is the record's own finding that every commit carried one identity, the account's, never a member's. The stripe names the two things the book says made the team safe to run. At a glance it is a familiar object rendered flat in teal, cream and vermilion; at arm's length the cream card on dark green is the strongest single shape of the three, with the title set across the foot.

## Recommendation

Concept 1, Open from the inside. It carries the whole argument in one image (an insider, a boundary, and a door opened from within by a team that meant no harm), it is the strongest thumbnail on a Leanpub shelf (one bright shape on a dark field, title in the top third where a small listing crops least), and it stays clear of attack imagery while still looking like a security book. Concept 3 is a close second and the easiest to read in a second; it loses because the badge says "insider" without saying "a team that governed itself". Concept 2 is the most elegant, but it is a book about a day before it is a book about the insider, and its strokes would need real data behind them to meet the rule that every number is traced.

## Notes for the conductor

- The RC dates the talk to 29 October 2026; the cover carries no date, so nothing needs changing, but the subtitle should not say 30 October.
- No vendor, model, logo, key, code or address appears on any cover. The only numerals are 00:16 and 19:00 on concept 2 and the "1" of the removable corner tag.
