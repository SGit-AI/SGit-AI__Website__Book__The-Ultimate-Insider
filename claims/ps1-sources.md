## 1. "Agent factory" in the industry

- **Microsoft:**
  - Microsoft has an "Agent Factory" blog series on the Azure blog. Its first post that I read, "Agent Factory: The new era of agentic AI: common use cases and design patterns", is dated 13 August 2025, by Yina Arenas (Corporate Vice President, Microsoft Foundry). It says the series "will share best practices, design patterns, and tools to help guide you through adopting and building agentic AI" (https://azure.microsoft.com/en-us/blog/agent-factory-the-new-era-of-agentic-ai-common-use-cases-and-design-patterns/, read).
  - Later posts in the series include "Creating a blueprint for safe and secure AI agents" (17 September 2025). That date is from listings, summary only.
  - Microsoft calls Foundry itself "an agent factory". That is also summary only.
- **Google:** Google Cloud's blog has "Level up your AI agent game with the Agent Factory" (https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/level-up-your-ai-agent-game-with-the-agent-factory). Summary only: not read, not dated.
- **What it supports:** the phrase is in use in the industry. There, it means a vendor platform for building and running many agents. The editor's sense is different: a team assembled fast around one objective and taken apart at the end. The book should say the phrase is shared and the meaning is his.
- **What it does not support:** that he coined the phrase, or that anyone used "factory of agents" first. I found no earliest use.

## 2. Bret Victor, "Inventing on Principle"

- **The talk:**
  - It was given at CUSEC (the Canadian University Software Engineering Conference) on 20 January 2012. That date comes from the title of the talk's own page on worrydream.com, as search returned it.
  - The page itself now redirects to the video (https://vimeo.com/906418692). I read neither the transcript nor the video.
- **What it is about, in summary:**

  - Victor's own principle is that creators need an immediate connection to what they create. He shows it with live-coding demos that give instant feedback.
- **What it supports:** the editor's "here he pressed play, and fine-tuned round after round" is that principle: an immediate connection to the medium. Quote Victor only from the video or a transcript read before print.

## 3. Kevin Kelly, "What Technology Wants"

- **The book:** published by Viking, New York, in October 2010. The author's page (https://kk.org/books/what-technology-wants, read) lists Viking hardcover and Penguin paperback. NPR's piece on it is dated 26 October 2010 (summary only).
- **Its idea:** Kelly calls the whole system of technology "the technium" and treats its development as a continuation of evolution, with tendencies he calls "wants". The author's page carries Matt Ridley's line that "technology's evolution is incremental, inevitable and inexorable": a blurb, not Kelly's words.
- **Reception:** mixed, in summary. Nature called the concept original. Reviews in American Scientist and the New York Times rejected technology having "wants" and called the account of evolution teleological. If the book leans on Kelly, it should say his idea is contested.
- **The editor's phrase, "the evolution of ideas":** not yet matched to a line of Kelly's. That needs the book, or Kelly's own essays, read.

## 4. Wardley maps: commoditisation

- **The evolution axis:** components move through "Genesis, Custom Built, Product, and Commodity", "the four predictable phases that all components in a value chain go through". The page adds that "All components eventually move toward Commodity stage", and "Evolution is inevitable".
  - Source: https://www.wardleymaps.com/evolution, read. The page says it was "adapted and expanded by Krzysztof Daniel" from Simon Wardley's work, under CC BY-SA 4.0, and was last reviewed on 17 August 2025.
- **Not yet read:** Simon Wardley's own text. His book chapters on Medium answered 403 from here.
- **What it supports:** the factory of agents as a step toward commodity: assembling a team becomes routine. The newsroom's own article of 31 October already uses it in this sense: "town-plan the villager service itself, into a commodity with a buy-now page" (see 5).
