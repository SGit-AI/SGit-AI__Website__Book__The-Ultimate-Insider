## Method

The talk's history at `298be4e` only: request and reply files (`team/*/requests/`), commit message bodies and their `To-<id>:` trailers, wake-record comments (including each record's `history`, which keeps a first verdict later overturned), debriefs and briefs. Two read-only sub-agents of this session picked candidates, one for the night and morning, one for the afternoon and evening; I checked a sample of the quotes word for word against the record (every one found). The mail itself lived in a public vault outside the repository and is seen here only through the trailers and the copies in `team/*/requests/`. No quote holds a key, a token value, a session or task id, or a contact detail.

Each row names the chapter it fits (by RC1's numbering): 01 the hold; 02 midnight; 03 mandates; 04 the hourly machine and the five-step wake; 05 the story and the register; 06 the editor signs; 07 building the talk; 08 the locks; 09 the team as insider. Times are the commit's, in UTC; where a file's own header gives a later time, the row says so.

## What the moments show together

Read in order, the quotes are a running argument about authority. Reviewers read the rules more strictly than they were written, and members obeyed and asked for the wording to change (13:08, 13:12). Members refused to act on a relayed word (studio at 17:41, the designer at 17:48) and refused to write in another member's file even when asked urgently (studio at 18:19), while the same fix went in through the editor's own word (dev at 18:18). The team also kept naming its own limits: "a second reading, not a second identity" at 02:19, "a scope is a setting, not a boundary" on a slide at 18:11. And it admitted its mistakes in public, small and large: a stylesheet name caught by a credential check, a gate finding lost in a shell pipe, a table that caused the bug it was meant to prevent.
