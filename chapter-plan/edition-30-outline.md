## Rules for every chapter

About six pages each. One map and one infographic per chapter (LK1), named below so the designer and ruv-sgit draw to the same page. A cast bar at each chapter's opening. Every scene banded "dramatised" with the moment it rests on. No figure in an agent's line; every figure by its claim id; the cost line stays C101 and C037. The editor's voice the default, the agents in turn. The running line's wording waits on the editor ("build" or "ship" something somebody else can use); use whichever he picks in every chapter.

## Chapter 1. The creator's problem (beat 1), in full

**Cast bar:** the editor alone; the nine shown greyed, not yet arrived.

**What it argues.** A creator's tools decide how much of the idea reaches the room. His slides were a compromise he talked over; the factory gave him a loop on his own medium: see it, ask for it differently, see it again. The book is about that factory, not the talk.

## Chapter 2. The factory is assembled (beat 2), in full

Outlined 2 November 2026, the 12:44 UTC wake. Midnight to dawn on 29 October: from an empty project to six agents on a clock, with the machinery that let the editor leave them alone.

**Cast bar:** the editor; the cards light as each agent arrives (dev, the conductor, deck, ruv-sgit within the hour; the researcher at about two; the designer before four). The storyteller, motion and studio stay greyed: they arrive in chapter 3.

**What it argues.** A factory is cheap to assemble when its parts already exist. Nothing the agents brought that night was new to the editor: the build, the check, the brief, the hold were all parts of earlier work. What was new was having them all at once, as a team, in hours (Wardley's commoditisation, shown before it is named). And the first thing the machine did was catch its own builder, which is the chapter's honest turn: the checks work, and they are not a wall.

**Pages and scenes, in order**

1. **The empty project** (about half a page). Narrator. A quarter past midnight, one empty project; by twenty to one, a brief and one page of rules. Keep from the ten, word for word: "A quarter past midnight: an empty project." The exact time once, on this page, by its claim (C001). *Dramatised* band: rests on the first commit and on dev's first pass, whose body names the brief, the reality and corrections files, one build and a gate (`1f7c633`).
2. **Four arrivals** (about one page). The four lines from the ten, kept as written (dev, the conductor, deck, ruv-sgit, each with its trait). Narrator between them on what each one owns: the site, the time, the slides, the workshop. The four arrive within the hour (C011: "arrived", their first commits, not their keys). Gloss here, at first use: **agent**, **conductor**, **hold**, **check**. Keep: "None of it was new." and "yesterday's experiment is today's part." The conductor's first roster names five agents, security among them, and security never came: one clause here, its empty chair set up for chapter 4 (`97b98d9`; `interviews/security.md`).
3. **The map, before its name** (about one page). The chapter's map, then one paragraph under it. Draw the factory as a chain: what the editor wanted (a talk the room understands) at the top; under it the slides, the site and the player; under those the agents; at the bottom the parts they ran on (the agent tool, the build, the check, the hold, the brief).
