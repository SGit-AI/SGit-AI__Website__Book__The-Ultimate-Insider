## How the full book grows from the seventy

The same five beats, in the same order, with the same cast bar, running line and margin clock (hour one to hour nineteen, CK1). Each of the seventy's parts roughly doubles. What the full book adds is the whole record, not new argument:

1. **Every hour of the day.** The seventy tells the scenes that carry the arc; the full book walks hours one to nineteen, so a reader can follow the margin clock through the day without a gap. The historian's day files (`timeline/day/`) and the register's hourly tables (C087, C095) are its spine; quiet hours get a short page, not a skip.
2. **Each agent at length.** The historian's exit interviews (`timeline/interviews/`) give each of the nine a page or two in the first person. **They are reconstructed from the record by the historian, not the agents' own words** (the interviews' own README): every claim in them cites a commit before `298be4e`, and the book marks each as the historian's reconstruction, never as an agent speaking. Any interviews the editor adds to the talk's repository after the day are cited separately, by commit, as added after the day.
3. **The talk as given.** The transcript, added after the day (`a9c0b49`), with the moments where the talk's pictures did the work; cited as after the day.
4. **The Cold-Read chapter** (D6-6): the second, blind team that read the same record without this book's briefs, and what it saw that this team did not (`timeline/cold-read-comparison.md`). In the full book only, not the seventy. The one-way valve holds: the chapter reads the blind team's published record; nothing from this repository goes to it.
5. **The appendices:** every number rerun (Appendix A, the register's commands), the control register (Appendix B), the making of this book and its dates.
6. **The record live online:** every `%% record:` marker becomes a link in the online edition (the augmented mode); in print, the "Where this came from" at each chapter's end.
