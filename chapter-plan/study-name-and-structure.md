The candidate names go to synthetic's personas, the researcher (the market words) and the publisher (the listing) before he chooses; their answers come into the second pass.

## What the memo changes

The signed plan braids two threads: the workflow is the main thread and the insider argument runs through it, because every control answers the risk. The memo keeps the first thread and moves the second out: "this cannot be a book about the inside agent ... I think that's a separate book". The centre of gravity becomes **a pragmatic, example-driven walkthrough of agentic workflows as they can be run in October 2026**: a multidisciplinary team kept aligned by shared protocols and open information, with explainability, provenance, determinism, project management and memory, and the plumbing (the non-functional requirements) built while making the talk. The test is the reader's: "Somebody should pick up this book and then know how to do all this by the time they finish it. Because remember, everything is open."

Three consequences for the structure:

1. **The spine stops being the clock.** RC1 follows the day hour by hour, which suits a story about one risky day. A walkthrough follows what a reader has to set up, in the order they would set it up. The day stays as the worked example every chapter returns to.
2. **The plumbing moves from the edge to the middle.** RC1's "What was already there" (11b) and the controls of chapters 3, 4 and 9 are the material the editor now calls "the story". They become the core chapters, not a look back.
3. **The insider argument becomes one chapter, honestly placed.** The failures stay in (the hold, the locks that never closed, the team as the insider); they are told as what the workflow taught, in one chapter on risk and its brakes, with the fuller argument left for its own book.

## The structure, proposed

Five parts and a back matter, each chapter one thing a reader can do, shown on the day and ended with **"Do it yourself"**: the open files to copy (this repository, the talk's, the tools), so the walkthrough is real. Built from RC1: almost every page moves rather than goes. Budgets are first estimates, to be balanced in the second pass; the total stays near D6-5's option A.

**The Gen ZX principles, applied** (`team/conductor/rc1/reviews/gen-zx-team.md`, sections 1 and 5): every concept explained where it first appears; maps taught first and built up (chapter 2, then one per part); parts opened with a picture; the making-of out of the reader's way; a reader able to do it by the end, which is the memo's own test.

## The name

What the memo asks of it: carry "agent", "agentic" or "agentic workflows"; say at a glance what the book does and why someone would buy it. What the market says (the conductor's Leanpub studies, `team/conductor/research/leanpub/03-the-market-on-leanpub.md`, read 30 October): the agentic shelf is crowded with books on **coding and engineering** with agents (Agentic Engineering, Agentic Programming, Agentic Coding, Harness Engineering, The Agentic AI Book). None is about **a team of agents working together on a real project, shown from its open record**. That is the gap the name should point at: a team, a workflow, a real record, open.

Rules for the candidates: no number in the title (every number goes through the register, and the title's earlier "ten agents" and "Nineteen Hours" contradicted it); no internal names; the subtitle says what the reader can do. "The Ultimate Insider" is kept for the separate book the editor names.

| # | Title | Subtitle | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| N1 | **Agentic Workflows, Shown Working** | how a team of agents built a talk in a day, and how to build yours | Says the category, the proof and the promise; "shown working" is the example-driven claim, and nobody else on the shelf can make it. |
| N2 | **The Agentic Team** | a walkthrough of agentic workflows, from an empty repository to a talk on stage | Puts the team first, which is the gap on the shelf; the subtitle says walkthrough and the arc. |
| N3 | **Agents That Work Together** | protocols, provenance and memory for a team of agents, on one open project | Plain words; names three of the five things the memo lists; "open project" says everything can be copied. |
| N4 | **Open Agentic Workflows** | how a multidisciplinary team of agents stays aligned, built on one real day | Leads with "open", the memo's last word and the book's strongest promise; "aligned" is the memo's collaboration point. |
| N5 | **The Plumbing of Agentic Teams** | the non-functional requirements that let a team of agents ship, walked through on a real project | The memo's "the story is the plumbing"; distinctive, but narrower: an engineer's title more than a leader's. |
| N6 | **Brakes So We Can Go Faster** | agentic workflows for a real team, walked through from its open record | Keeps the talk's best line as the hook, which carries the controls without making the book about the risk; the subtitle carries the required words. |

**Recommended for testing first: N1 and N2**, with N6 as the contrast (a hook title against a category title). The personas will say which one makes them pick the book up.
