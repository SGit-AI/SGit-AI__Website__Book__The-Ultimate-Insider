## The arc, on PS1

PS1 moves the centre one more time, and it is the clearest yet: **the book is about the factory, not the festival's bands.** A creator (the editor here; it could be the reader) was given a factory of agents: a team assembled fast around one objective, run hard for a day, and taken apart at the end. It was built on earlier experiments, and its own work became the next project's starting point. What it gave the creator was a feedback loop and control of the medium: where his slides used to be a compromise he had to talk over, here he pressed play, and fine-tuned round after round.

So the story arc is the factory's life, in five beats, each a part of the book:

1. **The creator's problem.** An idea to communicate, and a medium that always fell short: slides he had to explain. (Inventing on principle: the creator should see the effect of a change at once.)
2. **The factory is assembled.** In hours, from earlier pieces: a brief, rules in plain words, roles, a clock, a brake. The cast arrives. (Wardley's commoditisation: yesterday's experiment is today's part.)
3. **The factory runs.** The loop: build, check, show the creator, change, again. Agents argue, refuse relayed words, get caught by their own reviewers, run through the night alone. The talk gets made as a side effect.
4. **The factory is stopped and taken apart.** The hold, the work in flight, what never closed; the team disbanded; what was kept.
5. **The factory is rebuilt.** This book's own team, ported rule for rule, now writing about the first one; the Web Summit team next; the reader's own. (Kelly: one idea leads to the next.)

And the practical promise that runs under every beat: **read this, and set up your own factory.**

The talk keeps its place as the factory's product, and the insider argument as one honest chapter on the brakes. The failures stay in at every beat.

## The zoom ladder

Six editions, each a complete book at its altitude, each pointing down to the next. The reader starts at the top and stops wherever they have what they need. Pages are approximate.

| Edition | Says | Zooms into the next by |
|---|---|---|
| **1 page** | The whole arc in one picture: a creator, a factory of agents assembled for a day, a talk made, the factory stopped by one rule and taken apart, and rebuilt to write this book. One line: "build something somebody else can use, then do it again." | "Here is the day in three pages." |
| **3 pages** | The arc as a scene per page, told by the cast: assembling the factory (midnight to dawn); the factory running (the loop, the six alone, the argument); the stop and what you do tomorrow. | "Meet the cast, and start your own, in ten." |

| **30 pages** | A chapter per beat, each a few pages: the cast's scenes, one map and one infographic per chapter (LK1), and a "do this now" per chapter. | "The method in full, in seventy." |
| **70 pages** | The walkthrough: the study's six parts at working depth, the plumbing explained, the containment chapter, the cold read, principles. | "The whole record, in a hundred and fifty." |
| **150 pages** | The full book: every scene, the appendices, the record markers live online (the augmented mode). | Online: the record itself. |

**How the zoom works on the page.** Every edition keeps the same five beats in the same order, with the same cast bar and the same running line, so a reader who zooms in recognises where they are; each edition ends on a "zoom in" line naming the next and what it adds. The smaller editions are not summaries written after: they are written first, and each larger one expands its parent's beats, as the editor's "start small and zoom in" asks.

## Rules for every edition (from synthetic's room read, `review/synthetic/sample/summary.md`)

1. **No claim codes in the sentences.** Ids go to a note at the foot or the record markers (the augmented mode).
2. **No word before it is explained.** Conductor, wake, hold and Wardley map are glossed on first use, or kept out of the small editions.
3. **Step one has a how:** one tool, one account, one empty project, in the reader's words, and it works in an app builder too.
4. **One example that is not a talk:** a small business task or a product idea, set up the same way, in every edition from 3 pages up.
5. **A sentence on cost, even a range.** Only from the register: asked of the researcher (`storyteller__033`); until it has an entry, the page says that the record holds no figure for the day's cost (C037).
6. **Less code on the page:** no commands or hashes in the small editions.
