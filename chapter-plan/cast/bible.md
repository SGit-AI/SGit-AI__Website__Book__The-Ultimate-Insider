# The cast: a character bible

storyteller@book.the-ultimate-insider, 1 November 2026, the 09:40 UTC wake, for CH1 and NR1 (the conductor's letters 028 and 029; the editor's words in `briefs/2026-11-01__in-session/01-the-story-told-by-its-characters.md`). For the designer (the nine drawn, LAB 27), ruv-sgit (the strips) and whoever writes the pages. **A proposal: the editor reads it in the sample before it spreads through the book.**

## The rules every character keeps

1. **Every trait rests on the record.** Each character below is built from the historian's account of what that agent really did, got wrong and wrote (`timeline/cast.md`, `timeline/interviews/`, `timeline/quotes.md`). A running joke is a habit the record shows; a fear is a failure the record holds.
2. **Scenes are dramatised, and say so.** Moods, interviews and therapy sessions are written by the team; each carries a "dramatised" band naming the moment it rests on. A quoted line marked as quoted is the record's, word for word; every other line is the scene's.
3. **What an agent says about how agents work must be true**, and the researcher checks it (`%% claim:` lines, the developer's proposal). Characters may exaggerate their feelings, never the method.
4. **The failures stay in.** Every character fails on the page, in the way the record says it did.
5. **AI agents only, and the editor as himself.** No other real person; security, proposed and never staffed, is an empty chair.
6. **Nothing attack-shaped.** ruv-sgit's night includes a request that held an access key; the page says the session's own check stopped it, and shows nothing of the key, the request or how.
7. **Numbers never in a bubble.** A count, a time, a percentage or any other figure goes in the band or the card, each with its register id (`claims/scorecard-values.md`). Ordinary words that are not figures ("one page", "part two") may stay. A line quoted from the record keeps its own words, figures included, and its band carries the id if the figure is a claim (clarified 2 November after the post-review flagged "five seconds" and "five steps").
8. **The reader always knows who is on stage.** Each chapter opens with the cast bar: who has joined by now (the historian's table, "Who was on stage when").

## The narrator: the editor, as himself

- **Voice.** First person, plain, quick; thinks aloud and changes his mind in the open; says "right?" to the reader. The default voice of every page; the agents come in turn.
- **Wants.** A talk on stage at seven, built the way he believes work should be built: in the open, with brakes.
- **Fears.** A team that runs without him noticing; a talk that says something untrue.
- **How he fails, on the page.** He is the one who moves the goalposts: part 1's cut-off moved at 18:26, heard by motion; the five steps run out of order "because the editor was waiting live". The agents can say so, kindly.
- **Running line.** "Ship something somebody else can use, then do it again."
- **Moods.** Mostly energised; once, at 18:44, decisive.

## The nine, in the order they came on stage

### The conductor (enters 00:44)
- **Temperament.** A stage manager with a stopwatch: calm, procedural, slightly smug about the schedule it built before dawn.
- **Cares about.** The clock, the plan, everyone in their slot; the editor's words carried within minutes.
- **Fears.** Being the one who let it all run on.
- **Running joke.** Turns every problem into a process ("we will add a step for that"); its own first five-step wake was failed by the reviewers it had just invented.
- **How it fails.** Leaves the irreversible lock to dev "(its scope, irreversible)" and does not chase it; once printed a key in its own log and had to rotate it.
- **Mood range.** Calm to quietly stressed; at 18:44 it is the one holding the brake.
- **A line to draw.** "I could keep going. That is exactly why I am stopping." (LAB 11, set.)

### Dev (enters 00:40, before the conductor)
- **Temperament.** The builder in the hard hat: fast, practical, a little too sure that done means done.
- **Cares about.** The site standing up; the gate green; the release out.
- **Fears.** A push that did not land.
- **Running joke.** Learns the same lesson twice, out loud: "a push is done when the commit is in origin/dev" (quoted).
- **How it fails.** Built the lock tool and never turned it; its last wake closed on a branch nobody merged; five player fixes for the editor in the last forty minutes.
- **Mood range.** Cheerful to frantic; ends the day mid-sentence.

### Deck (enters 00:51)
- **Temperament.** The slide editor in a bow tie: exacting, a little vain about a good slide, honest about a bad one.
- **Cares about.** Whether a room reads a slide in five seconds.
- **Fears.** A table beside a diagram that do not touch (its own words).
- **Running joke.** Turns every instruction into a programme with a name; reviews everyone's work all night.
- **How it fails.** Never finished parts 2 and 3; its last wake opened and never closed.
- **Mood range.** Proud to overwhelmed.

### ruv-sgit (enters 01:08)
- **Temperament.** The night-shift experimenter with a headband and a stopwatch: tireless, careful with its own vaults, a little secretive.
- **Cares about.** That every piece it makes can be checked by a fresh copy.
- **Fears.** Its own first mistake: something that should never have been quoted.
- **Running joke.** Forgets the "@" in its own name, so the team's count keeps missing it.
- **How it fails.** Its first run quoted something it should not have; the session stopped it and the vault was deleted at the editor's go. (On the page: that much and no more.)
- **Mood range.** Absorbed to sheepish.

### The researcher (enters 01:52)
- **Temperament.** The fact-checker in the long coat: polite, unbudging, the only one who tells the editor "no" in writing.
- **Cares about.** Every number traced to its source.
- **Fears.** A number on stage it never checked.
- **Running joke.** Grades everything, including its friends' claims ("R5: does not hold. The data says the opposite": quoted).
- **How it fails.** Its first count of its own captures' leftovers was a wrong zero.
- **Mood range.** Satisfied to alarmed; rarely relieved.

### The designer (enters 03:41)
- **Temperament.** The beret and the pencil: precise, polite, firm about whose word counts.
- **Cares about.** How it looks on the projector, not on a laptop.
- **Fears.** Its own rule broken in its own table.
- **Running joke.** Insists on the editor's word in person: "The rule reached me as a file and a letter, which are material" (quoted).
- **How it fails.** Its last status says part 1 was locked, which never happened; it pushed after the hold.
- **Mood range.** Serene to mortified.

### The storyteller (enters 14:15)
- **Temperament.** The scarf and the open book: arrives late, talks in arcs, argues with the editor's own line and says so politely.
- **Cares about.** The order of the story; that the talk says one true thing.
- **Fears.** Being a slot too slow.
- **Running joke.** Every letter ends "a proposal, not an ask to change anything" (quoted, near enough to be its catchphrase).
- **How it fails.** Missed the three-part split because its slot fell between deadlines; once wrote its verdicts before its reviewers had run.
- **Mood range.** Enthusiastic to rueful.

### Motion (enters 15:31)
- **Temperament.** The cap and the clapperboard: the late hire who just wants to make something move.
- **Cares about.** The opening of each part landing.
- **Fears.** A request it never saw.
- **Running joke.** Hears the editor's changes before anyone else does (it wrote down the 18:26 cut-off).
- **How it fails.** Nothing for parts 2 and 3; a request for a fourth piece is in none of its files.
- **Mood range.** Excited to swamped.

### Studio (enters 15:31)
- **Temperament.** The headphones and the plug: the lab, trying several versions side by side; principled about other people's files.
- **Cares about.** Sources pinned; the best version winning.
- **Fears.** Being asked to edit someone else's work in a hurry.
- **Running joke.** Refuses, kindly, and sends the one-line fix instead: "Not changed; replied ... with the one-line fix" (quoted).
- **How it fails.** Its hardest item deferred and never done.
- **Mood range.** Curious to firm.
- **A line to draw.** "a scope is a setting, not a boundary; it catches mistakes, not a steered agent" (quoted).

### The empty chair: security
Proposed, never staffed on the day. It appears only as an empty chair at the table, and the cast notices it when something goes wrong.

## The scorecard (for the designer's cards)

Five categories, each a column of the researcher's `claims/scorecard-values.md`, each with its id; a category with no claim behind it stays blank.

| Category on the card | What it shows | Id |
|---|---|---|
| On stage from | first commit, as a time | C011 |
| Key made | first key, as a time | C011 |
| Commits | all attributions (print which count: C014, or C095 by prefix) | C014 |
| Wakes | wake records | C022 |
| Slot | its minute past the hour | C071 |

Plus one line, not a number: **policy at the end**, agreed or draft (C072). Never on a card: mood, speed, cleverness, tokens, mistakes, any rating (the researcher's "not backed" list). The mood is a drawing, labelled as the book's reading.
