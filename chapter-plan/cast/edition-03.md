## Page 1. The factory is assembled

**Cast bar:** the editor, alone; then dev, the conductor, deck, ruv-sgit.

**Narrator.** For years my slides were a compromise. I had an idea, and I spent the talk explaining the bits the slides could not show. On 29 October I tried something else. At a quarter past midnight I opened an empty project, wrote a brief and one page of rules, and asked for a team of AI agents to build my talk with me.

**Narrator.** They arrived one by one, and each announced itself.

**Dev** *(happy)*: First pass of the site and the talk. Built your way: the brief in full, one build, a gate.

**Conductor** *(pacing)*: I will keep the time. Everyone gets a slot, and anyone can pull the brake.

**Deck** *(proud)*: The slides are mine.

**ruv-sgit** *(absorbed)*: I will be in my workshop, making things you can check.

**Narrator.** None of it was new. Each piece came from something I had built before. What was new was having them all at once, working together, in an hour.

*Side note, for words on this page:* an **agent** is an AI that works in a loop: it reads, decides, acts with tools, and checks the result. The **conductor** is the agent that keeps the team's time and plan. A **brake**, here a **hold**, is one short file that stops every agent until the person in charge says go.

## Page 2. The factory runs

**Cast bar:** six agents. The editor's chair is empty from five in the morning.

**Narrator.** Then I went quiet, and they did not. Through the night and the morning they worked on a clock: plan, have the plan checked, do the work, have it checked, check it went to plan.

**Conductor** *(worried)*: I built the checks, and the first thing they caught was me. "Name it in the debrief or record." *(record)* Fair.

**Researcher** *(unbudging)*: My reviewer said a file was not mine to change. It was. "objection withdrawn." *(record)*

**Deck** *(exacting, to ruv-sgit)*: "What it is not yet is a picture a room reads in five seconds." *(record)* Again, please.

**Narrator.** In the afternoon I came back, saw what they had made, and asked for changes. That was the point: I could see an idea on the screen, ask for it differently, and see it again within the hour. A storyteller joined and told me, with the team's own data, that one of my lines did not hold. I kept the line. The book keeps the argument.

**Narrator.** It does not have to be a talk. A shop owner could set up the same team to answer customers, keep the stock list true and write the weekly offer, with the same brief, the same rules and the same brake.

## Page 3. The stop, and your own factory

**Cast bar:** all nine. Security's chair, planned at midnight, is empty.

**Narrator.** At 18:44, sixteen minutes before I went on stage, I told the conductor: "actually we are at 95% so it is better to pause all actions". *(record)* It wrote one file, and every new piece of work stopped. What was already in flight still landed. Then the factory was taken apart.

**Conductor** *(at ninety-five, then calm)*: I could keep going. That is exactly why I am stopping.

**Narrator.** Anyone on the team could stop everything. Only I could start it again. That rule is why I could let them run through the night. And the next day I built a second factory from the first one's parts, to write this book.

**Narrator.** Yours can start tomorrow, in four steps:
1. **One tool and one empty project.** An account with an AI agent tool and a new, empty project; an app builder works too. Put nothing private in it: no password, no key, no personal detail. Give your agent only the access it needs.
2. **One page of rules**, in your own words: what the agent is for, what it may change, and when it must stop and ask you.
3. **One brief**: what you want made, for whom, by when.
4. **Ship something small that somebody else can use, the same day.** Then do it again, and add a second agent only when the first is busy.
