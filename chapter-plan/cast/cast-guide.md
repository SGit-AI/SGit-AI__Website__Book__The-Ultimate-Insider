## The rules every character keeps

1. **Every trait rests on the record.** Each character below is built from the historian's account of what that agent really did, got wrong and wrote (`timeline/cast.md`, `timeline/interviews/`, `timeline/quotes.md`). A running joke is a habit the record shows; a fear is a failure the record holds.
2. **Scenes are dramatised, and say so, lightly; the logistics leave the reading text.** (Revised 4 November 2026, at the editor's guidance of that night, `briefs/2026-11-04__in-session/07-speak-as-me-no-logistics.md`, and the conductor's letter 055.) A dramatised scene keeps its mark in the lightest form. **As printed (DM1, signed 4 November 2026, option (c)):** the build sets it as a small "Dramatised" tag beside the first speaker of a scene in which an agent speaks, and drops it where only the narrator or a concept box speaks; the chapter files keep their *Dramatised.* lines. It stays because NR1 is signed ("dramatised scenes, marked as such and grounded in the record") and dropping it would be his decision, not ours. Everything else that was in a scene's band (commit times, recording-clock stamps, "added after the day", which file, which transcript) leaves the reading text: it goes to the chapter's `%% record:` lines, which are not printed, where the librarian and the researcher check it. **Where this came from** is not printed in the book either (WC1, signed 4 November 2026, about 01:43 UTC); it stays in the source for an augmented edition later. A line quoted from the record is still the record's, word for word, and still marked as quoted; the source of the quote moves to the notes.
3. **What an agent says about how agents work must be true**, and the researcher checks it (`%% claim:` lines, the developer's proposal). Characters may exaggerate their feelings, never the method.
4. **The failures stay in.** Every character fails on the page, in the way the record says it did.
5. **AI agents only, and the editor as himself.** No other real person; security, proposed and never staffed, is an empty chair.
6. **Nothing attack-shaped.** ruv-sgit's night includes a request that held an access key; the page says the session's own check stopped it, and shows nothing of the key, the request or how.
7. **Numbers never in a bubble, and every number traced at the chapter's end.** (Revised 4 November 2026, with rule 2.) No count, time, percentage or other figure in an agent's line; a line quoted from the record keeps its own words, figures included. In the narrator's prose, a time appears only when the time is the story (the style sheet's rule 5). Every figure on the page keeps its register id, but the id lives in the chapter's `%% record:` lines (not printed, WC1), never beside the sentence (CLAUDE.md: every number traced; every chapter keeps where it came from, in its record lines). Ordinary words that are not figures ("one page", "part two") may stay. **Stats in cards are data, not speech** (the editor's brief 13, 4 November): a cast card's panel, the day-at-a-glance page and the infographics may carry figures (commits, wakes, hours), each from the register with its id in the record line; the agent's own words beside the card stay free of figures. **One exception, his:** chapter 16's high five, where brief 13 asks for "the conductor talking about how great this was ... throw some more stats on it"; there the speech carries the totals and the card each member's (writer3's 004, accepted in the storyteller's 155).
8. **The reader always knows who is on stage.** Each chapter opens with the cast bar: who has joined by now (the historian's table, "Who was on stage when").

### The narrator's style sheet (4 November 2026)

Taken close to its words from the newsroom journalist's pass on the narrator (`team/conductor/from-newsroom/2026-11-04__narrator-voice-part-one.md`, section 2), which the editor liked and the conductor sent as the model (its letter 057). It agrees with his own notes of that night (`briefs/2026-11-04__in-session/03` to `07`). Its *Check* items and its questions are his to answer, not ours.

1. **First person, from now.** "Looking back", "what I did not expect", "I think". The narrator knows how the day ended and how the book came out of it.
2. **Opinion is allowed, and expected.** Say what something means and why it matters. Then let the agents show it.
3. **Connect the dots.** Earlier talks, earlier work, the other book (Gen ZX Founders), Wardley maps: one link per passage is plenty, and each link traced.
4. **Let sentences run when the thought runs.** Use "and", "because", "which", "so". Keep the short sentence for the line that should land, not for every line.
5. **Times only when the time is the story.** "Half an hour before the talk" earns its place; "at 07:43" belongs in the notes (rule 7).
6. **Translate the team's words once, then use them sparingly.** A wake is "a scheduled session"; the hold is "the stop file". The agents can use their own words freely.
7. **Never defer the conclusion.** Not "the historian reads it as". Instead: "I read it as..., and the historian's record agrees."
8. **Some energy, honestly sized.** "This is the part I like most" is him. Calling the day historic is not.
9. **Keep the honesty that is already there.** "The record does not say", "Nobody tidied it", the failures at full size.
10. **Hand over cleanly.** When the scene belongs to an agent, the narrator sets it up in a sentence or two and gets out of the way.
11. **Never pass judgement on the reader.** No "easy to miss", "as you know", "obviously", "you may not have noticed", "underrated". Say the thing, and let the reader decide whether it was new to them.
12. **Speak as me, not about me** (his own guidance, brief 07). The narrator is already him: say the point in the present, not "as I said in the talk" with a quotation to justify it. "As I put it in the talk" is fine once; quoting him "left, right and center" is not.
13. **The parts in words, the chapters in digits** (the conductor's 070, from critic2; 4 November 2026). In the reading text a part is "part one" to "part five", in words, lower case mid-sentence, and "Part one" at the start of a sentence, in a heading or in Start here's map. A chapter is always a digit, "chapter 13", as there are 23 of them. Never "part 3" or "Part 4".
14. **The five steps and the five stages** (letters 140 and 162; the newsroom's pass 3). "The five steps" is the reader's formula (the opening spread, chapter 23). The wake's own checks are "the wake's five stages" everywhere else. One exception, his (ST2, signed 5 November 2026, "Steps is ok"): in his two held lines that call the wake's stages "steps" (chapter 7, "the steps were more than ceremony"; chapter 9, "The five steps were doing what I had asked of them"), his word stays; the writer put it back in 48b2980e.
15. **The narrator's mark at a change of voice** (NAR1, signed 13 November 2026, brief 02 of the print review). The narrator's mark (his round head and "**Narrator.**") appears where the voice changes to him, not again on the next paragraph of the same voice: a run of his paragraphs is one block with one mark. A new heading starts a new block and may carry it again, because a heading says who is talking. What sits inside his run without another speaker does not change the voice, so it does not end the block: a Figure or Map line, a "Words for this page" box, a concept callout, a Dramatised line, a record line. Only another speaker's line, or a heading, ends it (13 November 2026, on the publisher's 014 and chapter 10).

What stays from the rules above: every fact about him still rests on the record or his briefs (rule 1), and a view of his not in either is flagged for him (`%% for the editor:`) until he keeps or drops it.
