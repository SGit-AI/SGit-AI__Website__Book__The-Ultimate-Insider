## The argument with a reviewer

**Clock:** Hour five.

`%% clock record: 04:11; clock hours of 29 October (C110; the conductor's CK1).`

`%% for the editor: "and it is my favourite" is the writer's reading of his view; his to keep or drop.`

**Narrator.** The first one is small, and it is my favourite. Just after four, the researcher planned to write a file of my decisions. Its reviewer read the plan and said no: that file, it said, was outside the researcher's rules.

**Researcher** *(unbudging)*: My reviewer said a file was not mine to change. I showed it was.

**Researcher** *(pointing at its own page of rules)*: It sits in my evidence folder. My rules let me write there. I am not asking for an exception; I am asking you to read the line again.

*Reviewer (the clipboard):* "objection withdrawn." *(record)*

**Researcher** *(already back at work)*: Good. Then it goes in, and the dispute goes in the record with it.

**Narrator.** An agent disagreed with its own checker, made its case from the rules, and won, at four in the morning, with nobody to appeal to. And the checker wrote down that it had been wrong.

## Sent back, and owned

**Narrator.** Three more, through the morning. Each is a member of the team telling another one, or itself, that something is not good enough, in writing.

**Narrator.** Later, at about twenty past six, the workshop. ruv-sgit had made a picture for the talk from real data. Deck, who owns the slides, reviewed it.

**Deck** *(exacting, to ruv-sgit)*: "What it is not yet is a picture a room reads in five seconds." *(record)*

**Deck** *(gentler)*: The data is honest. The table and the diagram sit side by side and never touch. A room will not do that work for you.

**ruv-sgit** *(sheepish)*: Back to the workshop.

**Deck** *(a little put out, then fair)*: One at a time, then. I will wait until the first one comes back.

**Narrator.** Third, the designer. A rule in its own design table had sent dev the wrong sizes for some text, and dev had built exactly what the table said.

**Designer** *(mortified)*: "The miss is my table's as much as the build's" *(record)*. Dev built what I specified. I specified it wrong.

## The objection kept

**Clock:** Hours fifteen to sixteen.

`%% clock record: 14:33 and 15:02; clock hours of 29 October (C110; the conductor's CK1).`

**Narrator.** One thing I asked for came back as a no.

**Narrator.** My story for the talk leaned on a claim: that the outside of an organisation, the part facing the internet, is more mature than its inside. Soon after the storyteller arrived, the researcher checked it against the published breach data and found the opposite.

**Researcher** *(to the new storyteller)*: "does not hold. The data says the opposite" *(record)*

**Storyteller** *(rueful)*: Then it goes to the editor, as the strongest objection to his own part one. Not buried.

**Storyteller** *(to itself, a slot later)*: I could soften it and keep the story smooth. But a story that hides the strongest argument against it is not one I want to tell. He decides; I just make sure he sees it.

**Narrator.** It came to me. I had already kept the line, two minutes before, in my decisions of three o'clock; the record has no answer of mine to the objection itself. The line stayed in the talk, and the researcher's objection went on the record beside it. The book keeps both.

## Do this now

**Narrator.** You will not get a loop by planning one. You get it by handing something over and listening to what comes back.

*Give one small thing to somebody else today, and write down what they asked for differently. Then change it, and give it to them again.*
