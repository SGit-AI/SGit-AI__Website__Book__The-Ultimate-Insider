# The factory is assembled

**Cast bar:** the editor; then dev, the conductor, deck and ruv-sgit, each card lighting as it arrives; the researcher at about two, the designer before four. The storyteller, motion and studio stay greyed until chapter 3.

## The empty project

**Narrator.** A quarter past midnight: an empty project. Not a template, not a half-built site from last time. A new repository with a licence, a short read-me and nothing else. Its first commit says "Initial commit", and that is all it says.

**Narrator.** By twenty to one it held a brief and one page of rules. The brief said what the talk was for, who it was for and when it was due; the rules said what the team was for, what each member could touch, and when it had to stop and ask me. The first agent had read both and was building.

## Four arrivals

*Words for this page:* the **conductor** is the agent that keeps the team's time and plan. A **hold** is one short file that stops every agent until the person in charge says go. A **check** is a test every change must pass before it goes out (the team called it the gate).

**Dev** *(happy, the fast one)*: First pass of the site and the talk. One build, and a check every change must pass.

**Dev** *(already typing)*: I put the brief up in full, where everyone can read it. And beside it, what is real, and what we got wrong. We will need that last one.

**Narrator.** Dev owns the site. It was the first to arrive, and the first thing it did was make a place where every later change would be tested before it went out.

**Conductor** *(pacing)*: I keep the time. Everyone gets a slot, and anyone can pull the hold.

**Conductor** *(reading its own roster)*: I have named who we need. Security is on the list. Its chair is ready.

**Narrator.** The conductor owns the clock. Its first act was a list of who the team needed, and one name on that list never arrived. Remember the empty chair: it comes back at the end.

**Deck** *(proud, the fussy one)*: The slides are mine. If a room cannot read one at a glance, it goes back.

**Deck** *(to nobody in particular)*: The beats, the script, the slides. In that order. A slide with no beat behind it is decoration.

**Narrator.** Deck owns the talk itself: its beats, its words and its slides.

**ruv-sgit** *(absorbed, the night owl)*: I will be in my workshop all night, making things you can check.

**ruv-sgit** *(not looking up)*: First run is open. An article drafted, waiting for the editor's check. Nothing goes out on my word alone.

**Narrator.** ruv-sgit owns the workshop: the experiments that might become pictures for the talk. It was the fourth to arrive, and it arrived already working.

**Narrator.** Four agents, all within the hour of an empty project. The build, the check and the brief came from things I had built before, on other projects. The hold was made that night. What was new was having them all at once, as a team, in one night. That is how a factory gets cheap: yesterday's experiment is today's part.

## The map, before its name

**Figure:** the factory as a chain. At the top, what I wanted: a talk the room understands. Under it, the slides, the site and the player. Under those, the agents. At the bottom, what the agents ran on: the agent tool, the build, the check and the brief, all at the ordinary end; and the hold, at the new end, made that night. Each part placed left to right by how settled it already was, from new on the left to ordinary on the right. The chapter's map; its owner agreed by the conductor.

**Narrator.** Look at the bottom row first. Almost all of it was already ordinary to me that night. I already worked with an agent tool every day. The build and the check were the way my sites are built; dev's first commit calls it the sgit.ai way. The brief and the page of rules were habits. Two things were new: the top of the picture, this talk, these slides, this player; and one part at the bottom, the brake.

**Narrator.** This kind of picture is called a Wardley map. It shows what a thing is made of, and how settled each part is. The point of this one fits in a line: the bottom of the chain was already ordinary, so the top could be built in a day. And the one new part at the bottom was the control: the brake that let me leave it running.

*Words for this page:* a **Wardley map** is a picture of what something depends on, top to bottom, with each part placed by how new or how ordinary it is.

## The clock and the five steps

**Narrator.** Then, in the small hours, the conductor built the machinery. This is the part that let me leave them alone, and I did not know yet how much I would.

**Conductor** *(on the minute)*: A clock: each member wakes at its own minute. A hold anyone can pull. And every piece of work goes the same way: plan it, have the plan checked, do it, have it checked, check it went to plan.

**Conductor** *(to the team, a little smug)*: Nobody waits to be told. When your minute comes round, you look at what is yours and you do the next thing.

*Words for this page:* a **wake** is one turn of work by one agent, on its own clock. A **reviewer** is a second agent that reads the plan before the work and the result after it. A **debrief** is the short note an agent writes at the end of each wake.

**Narrator.** Five steps, in plain words: plan it; have the plan checked; do it; have it checked; check it went to plan. Every member, every wake. If a check said stop, the work stopped. It looks slow written down. The checking is what let nobody watch.

**Figure:** the five-step wake as a loop, with a clock round it: each member's minute marked on the dial. The chapter's infographic, for the designer. The minutes are in the picture, not in any agent's mouth.

## Caught by its own reviewer

**Narrator.** One thing to know before this scene: the reviewer is not a different kind of thing. It is the same kind of agent, in the same session, given a checker's brief instead of a builder's.

**Narrator.** The conductor ran the five steps on its own first piece of work, inside a few minutes of writing them. Its reviewer found against it: the conductor had changed a file outside its own page of rules. I had allowed it, but the commit did not say so, and the plan had said it must.

*Reviewer (a second agent with a checker's brief, drawn as a clipboard):* "Name it in the debrief or record." *(record)*

**Conductor** *(worried, then calm)*: Caught by the reviewer I invented. Good. But know what it is: "a second reading, not a second identity." *(record)*

**Conductor** *(writing it down)*: The first wake closes partial. That goes in the record too.

**Narrator.** I keep coming back to that line. A reviewer agent in the same session can catch mistakes. It cannot stop someone set on getting round it. We built the checks knowing that.

## Before dawn

**Narrator.** Two more arrived before it was light. The researcher at about two: the one who checks every fact against its source, and the one who would tell me "no" in writing before the day was out. Then the designer, a little before four, with the first design pass.

**Narrator.** Their cards light here. You will hear from both of them in the next chapter.

**Narrator.** So by four in the morning there were six agents, a clock and a brake. A factory that could run without me. From just after four, it did.
