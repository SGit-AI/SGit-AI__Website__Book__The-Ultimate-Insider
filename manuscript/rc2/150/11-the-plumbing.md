# The plumbing

**Cast bar:** all of them, all day: this chapter is about what ran under every scene.

`%% for the editor: "Nobody puts the plumbing in the brochure." is the writer's, and the newsroom keeps it as his; it prints as a figure of speech, not a view (storyteller__150). "One of the most important choices of the day" is now held, below.`

**Narrator.** Nobody puts the plumbing in the brochure. But every story in this book ran through four pipes, and if you build your own factory you will need all four: a register for every number, a check every change passes, a site to work on, and a brief with a file beside it for what turned out to be true. The register first.

**Figure:** the plumbing as a building's services: the site, the check, the register and the brief, each drawn as a pipe running into the factory floor.

## Every number traced

**Narrator.** The talk made claims. Numbers about insiders, about breaches, about what agents do. Every one of them went into a register: a file listing the claim, where it came from, the captured source, and a grade for how well the source supports it. By early evening the register held over three hundred claims. Most held, some only in part, some had not been checked yet, and three did not hold.

**Researcher**: A claim without a captured source is a gap, and the register says so rather than leaving it out. Gaps are allowed. Hiding them is not.

**Narrator.** None of the three made it into the talk. It was not luck, it was the register doing its job.

## The check that keeps it true

**Narrator.** Every change to the site went through the same check before it could go out, and if anything on its list failed, the change stopped. The card has the list.

**Figure:** a card, what the check looked at that first night: every link resolves; every page has its plain-text twin; no code loaded from elsewhere; every script parses; every beat has a slide; no long dash in the prose; a tripwire over every file for anything that looks like a secret.

**Narrator.** The check had a twin, in words: the team's first page of rules, from chapter 4. Put the two side by side and half of the page is the check said in words. The other half is what no check could hold.

`%% Hold (P30: an opinion in his voice, the newsroom's; prints once he confirms; storyteller__150): "... and that is the half I find more interesting, because it is where trust lives."`

**Narrator.** The check does not care who made the change, or how sure they were. A member that is certain its work is fine still has to pass the same test as one that is not.

**Narrator.** Chapter 5 has the example I like best: on the first night, the check stopped the release of the very member that had built it.

## The site is the scaffolding

**Narrator.** The agents did not work in a chat window. They worked on a website: every slide a page, every member a page, every session and every commit a row on a page.

`%% Hold (P30: an opinion in his voice, the newsroom's; prints once he confirms; storyteller__150): "... and I think it was one of the most important choices of the day."`

`%% Check (the newsroom, part three, chapter 11; prints once the researcher registers the earlier talks as sites and vaults and he confirms): "It is the same choice I made for the newsroom, and for the talks before this one: if the work is a site, it has an address, a history and a check."`

**Narrator.** A website gives an agent somewhere to put things that stays put. Its work is a file with a history, not a message that scrolls away. And a site can show the team its own state: the dashboard, the wakes page, the release history, all built from the files the agents wrote. The site remembered for them, and it showed me what they had done, on pages built from their own files.

**Important concept: the repository growing.** The site was the repository, and it grew every hour: 53 files at the end of the first hour, 1,313 at the day's last release, and its pages from 23 to 163. The biggest jump in pages came in the afternoon hour when the last two agents joined, the two you meet in chapter 14. Nothing was tidied away as it grew. What changed was the design around it: pages built from the team's own files, one frame for every page, and side navigation.

**Figure:** the repository at the end of each hour, files by kind.

## The brief and its corrections

**Narrator.** The last pipe is the simplest, and on the day it was the one that did not quite work.

**Narrator.** The brief was my request, published in full and never edited. Beside it sat two files, one card each.

**Figure:** a card, the reality file: kept current by whoever shipped; what exists now and nothing else; changed all day, last in the evening. Its second sentence across the top: "A thing not listed here does not exist, whatever a brief or an article says."

**Figure:** a card, the corrections file: for when the brief turns out to be wrong. Under its heading and a line of purpose, one entry, unchanged from the first half hour to the end of the day.

`%% figure brief: For the designer. Two cards side by side, the reality file's crowded with dated lines, the corrections file's nearly empty.`

**Narrator.** At the end of the day, the corrections file still said what it said in the first half hour:

> "None yet." *(record)*

**Narrator.** The brief was wrong in places. It says the talk is "tomorrow"; by the time the project existed, it was that same day. And the talk it asks for became something quite different in the afternoon, by my own decisions. None of that went into the corrections file. It went into the reality file, the decisions and the debriefs instead. Nobody used it, me included.

`%% Hold (P30: an opinion in his voice, the newsroom's; prints once he confirms; storyteller__150): "The rule was right, and nobody used it, me included."`

**Narrator.** All of that ran under every scene, and for nine hours it ran without me. In hour fourteen, I came back.

## Something to try

*One check that every change has to pass is where all of this begins. If you try this, start with the one that would have saved you last time: a check that every link works, say, or that no file holds a password.*
