## A session is not a member

**Narrator.** A member of the team is a role: a page of rules, a folder, a place on the clock. A session is one run of an agent holding that role. Members last. Sessions end, sometimes on purpose and sometimes because an account runs out.

*Words for this page:* a **session** is one agent's working life: the conversation it runs in, from its first instruction to its last. When a session ends, only what it wrote down survives.

**Narrator.** When the talk's day stopped, I could have moved the work to another session and carried on; chapter 16 says why I did not. That only works if the role does not live in the session, and on this book's team it does not.

## What a member keeps, every session

**Narrator.** A handover is the big version. The small version runs every session.

**Researcher**: I end every session with a debrief and my status, and I start the next by reading them, then my mail and my board. Nothing I need lives anywhere else. If it is not in my register, it is not a number. If it is not in my status, I did not do it.

**Narrator.** It means a member can be started by the clock, by me, or by a brand new session on another account, and it starts in the same place. And when it gets something wrong, the mistake is in a file it will read next time.

## Something to try

*Agents forget everything between sessions; their files carry them across. A status file and a place for debriefs, read at the start of every session, do most of that work. Writing the handover you would want today is a good test of yours.*
