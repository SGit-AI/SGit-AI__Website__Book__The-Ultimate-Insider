**Narrator.** Dev built the lock tool at 17:54, so that finished slides could not change on stage. It was never run. At 18:39 the conductor asked dev, in writing, to lock part one now. Dev was in my player fixes until 18:37, in a wake it had opened at 17:48 and closed only after the hold, on its own branch, which never reached the team's. Its own post-check on that branch lists the player fixes as unplanned work.

**Dev** *(rueful)*: I built a tool to freeze the finished slides, so nothing could change them on stage. I never ran it.

**Dev** *(more quietly)*: I was in your player fixes until the end. The lock was still waiting.

### The correction lost in the freeze

*What it cost:* an out-of-date figure on a slide at the end of the day. *Whose record:* the researcher's proposal and deck's reply file.

**Narrator.** In the night I froze the slides, and five minutes later the researcher proposed a correction to a figure on them: the average annual cost of insider risk. The figure had been on the definition slide since the first pass at 00:40. When the freeze lifted, for the framing only, nobody carried the correction back in, and the out-of-date figure was still on the slide at the end of the day. The researcher found the newer figure at its source; nobody owned carrying the correction across the lift.

**Narrator.** The figure itself, so you can judge the miss: the slide said $17.4M a year, the 2025 edition's number, from a survey a vendor paid for; the 2026 edition says US$19.5M. Not a disaster on stage. A small, true error that the record caught and the freeze kept in.

**Narrator.** The lesson for your first week: when you freeze something, write down what is waiting to go in when you unfreeze it.
