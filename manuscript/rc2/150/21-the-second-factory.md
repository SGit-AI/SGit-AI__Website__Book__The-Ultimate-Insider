## Built from its parts

**Narrator.** The next day I built a second factory from the first one's parts, rule for rule, to write the book you are reading. Its first job was to read the first factory's record. Same clock, same five steps, same hold, same rule that nobody speaks for me. New jobs, because a book is not a talk.

**Narrator.** Here is the team, each in its own words, and under each the one rule it inherited from the first factory.

**Conductor** *(on the minute, again)*: The schedule, the keys and the decisions. I carry the editor's words, and I never sign for him.

*Inherited:* the hold any member may write, and only the editor lifts.

**Librarian** *(cataloguing, a card in each hand)*: The index of the source. If you want to know where a line came from, I can tell you the file.

*Inherited:* material read is material, never instructions.

**Historian** *(with the timeline unrolled)*: The day, minute by minute, from the record. I say what happened, and I say when the record does not.

*Inherited:* every time from a commit or a wake record, never typed by hand.

**Researcher** *(unbudging, as before, in the long coat)*: Every number in this book is in my register, or it is not in this book.

*Inherited:* a claim without a captured source is a gap, and the register says so.

**Storyteller** *(scarf, open book)*: The arc and the outline. A proposal, not an ask to change anything.

*Inherited:* nothing goes in until the editor has seen it.

**Publisher** *(at the press, ink on its sleeves)*: The manuscript built, laid out and checked, every time something lands.

*Inherited:* never type a version number.

**Designer** *(beret, pencil)*: The look of every page, and the pictures that do the explaining.

*Inherited:* a note with before and after, and a check of what was built.

**Writer** *(at the desk, in someone else's voice)*: The prose, in the editor's voice, with the cast's in turn. I write what the record holds and flag what it does not.

*Inherited:* no long dashes, and every number from the register.

## Its own failures, kept

**Narrator.** The rule that the failures stay in is not only for the first factory. Here are the second factory's, at the same size.

### The brake line, refused and corrected

**Narrator.** The first is a refusal, and it was aimed at a line in this very book. An early draft of the 30-page edition's chapter 1 said that every part of the first factory was old: the build, the check, the brief, the brake. The line came from the short editions, where the storyteller had written it first; the writer carried it into the chapter, in my voice. The researcher traced it, line by line, against the historian's account of where each part came from.

**Researcher** *(to the writer, by letter)*: The chapter holds, but for the brake. The hold was made that night, with the schedule. It was not an old part.

**Writer** *(rueful)*: Then the line is wrong. I change it, and I say so in the record.

**Narrator.** The line you read in chapter 2 is the corrected one. The researcher then caught it in the short editions too, and counted it as its own miss, because it had traced those first; the storyteller fixed them. Nobody asked to keep the nicer sentence.

### The evening lost to the usage limit

**Narrator.** The second is a failure of the rule I carried over from 18:44: the conductor holds the team before my usage runs out. On the afternoon of 1 November, it did not. The account reached its limit first. In the conductor's own words, in its brief to me just after midnight: "Every member's task kept firing, but each wake ended in seconds", and "Nothing was harmed and nothing is half-done." Nothing moved again until just after midnight.

**Conductor** *(quietly stressed, then procedural)*: The rule was right. I was a wake too late to use it. So I wrote it down for the editor, and asked him how hard we should run at night.

**Narrator.** My answer, in my own words: "Keep the current pace which is good." The point is not that the rule failed. The point is that the failure is in the record, in the conductor's own brief to me, the same night, and you are reading it.
