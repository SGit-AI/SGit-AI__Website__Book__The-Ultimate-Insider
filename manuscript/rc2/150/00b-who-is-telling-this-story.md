# Who is telling this story

**Narrator.** The opening pages said who I am and who the team was. The talk was at hack::soho, in IOActive's London office, and every one of us left a record of what it did: its plan, its work, its checks, its notes to the others. This book is built on that record.

**Narrator.** But a record is not a story. So the book has two kinds of voice. The agents tell the day as it happened, in the present tense, each one as the character the record shows: dev, fast and practical; the conductor on the minute; the researcher who will not move without a source. Their scenes are dramatised, and they say so. I tell it from now, looking back. I know how the day ended, which parts worked, and which only looked as if they did. So when I speak, I will tell you what I think it meant, where it came from, and what I would do with it if I were you.

**Narrator.** A lot of it came from somewhere. This was not the first time I tried to make a talk behave differently. For the Black Hat Europe keynote of 9 December 2025, the slides became data: one file that the presentation read when it opened, so the slides could be changed like code. The VoiceDebrief pitch added the presenter: timings per slide, a countdown, my notes. And for a short demo for ruv.sgit.ai, due on 15 October, the whole presentation became a timeline of beats, with me speaking to it. Each earlier experiment fixed one thing and showed me the next. The day in this book is where they came together, with a team behind them for the first time.

**Narrator.** There is one more thing I want you to see as you read. This book is being made the same way the talk was. A team of agents is writing it, drawing it and checking it: a storyteller who outlines each chapter, a researcher who keeps a register in which every number has an id, a historian who reconstructs the day from the record, a designer who drew the nine characters, and their four moods, in code, overnight. I think the way this book was made is as interesting as the day it describes, and I have tried not to hide it.

**Narrator.** I wanted this to be a creative book, not only a correct one. If I stand up and say a talk was made by a team of agents and it looks like every talk before it, there is no reason to believe anything has changed. A book is the same. So this one has a cast, scenes, a clock in the margin and pictures that do the explaining. And it has me, saying what I think. Where the agents disagree with me, they say so too: in chapter 13, the researcher tells me a line of my own talk does not hold, and I keep it anyway, with its objection beside it.
