**Cast bar:** seven agents lit, the storyteller newest; the researcher beside it; the editor back.

## The story before the slides

**Clock:** Hour fifteen.

**Narrator.** The storyteller joined at a quarter past two, and the first thing it did was the thing I had asked for in my feedback round and not done myself: it put the story before the slides. ### The tree and the maps, as they were used

**Narrator.** The tree put the talk's beats in an order with a reason behind each one: what the room must believe first, and what follows from it. It is the shape of an argument, drawn before anyone drew a slide for it.

**Figure:** the storyteller's story tree as it was that afternoon: the talk's beats as branches, as its own text has them, with no turn marked, since the tree marks none at 14:19 (df37797); a later version, after my decisions of 15:00 (2afe0d7, 16:04), labels one edge as the turn.

**Narrator.** The maps did something different. They put the parts of my argument where they sit relative to each other: what depends on what, and how settled each piece is. The slide that carried one said, in small type, "positions, not measurements". That line is the honest heart of a map like that: it shows you where things are, not how big they are.

**Figure:** one of the storyteller's maps as it went into the talk, with its small line, "positions, not measurements".

## The objection kept

**Clock:** Hours fifteen and sixteen.

`%% clock record: 14:33, 15:02 and 15:16; clock hours of 29 October (C110; the conductor's CK1).`

**Narrator.** My story for the talk leaned on a claim: that the outside of an organisation, the part facing the internet, had become, in the outline's words for R5, "smaller and more mature than it was". The claim compared the outside with itself, then and now. The storyteller asked the researcher for the facts behind its beats, and the researcher checked that claim against the published breach data, and found the opposite.

**Researcher** *(to the new storyteller)*: "does not hold. The data says the opposite" *(record)*

**Narrator.** That is the researcher doing what it is for. It could not change a slide. It could only say, in writing, that a line did not hold, with the source beside it.

`%% record: 2726f0e (14:33; R5: the surface smaller and more mature than it was) C030 (story-r5-surface-smaller-more-mature: "no source for 'smaller'")`
`%% band: Dramatised (the researcher's line). The quoted words are its finding's own.`
*Dramatised.*

**Narrator.** Then the storyteller had a choice. The finding contradicted a line of mine that the storyteller's own tree depended on. At 15:02 it folded the researcher's facts into the tree and put the finding to me, as the strongest objection to my own part one, in a letter.

**Storyteller** *(rueful)*: Then it goes to the editor, as the strongest objection to his own part one. Not buried.

**Storyteller** *(to itself)*: He decides; I just make sure he sees it.

`%% record: b208a16 (15:02:48, the storyteller's pass and its letter) team/storyteller/story/challenge.md at 298be4e`
`%% band: Dramatised. The storyteller's lines rest on its letter of 15:02 only; the words are ours.`
*Dramatised.*

**Narrator.** It came to me, and I answered it in my answers of that afternoon, committed at 15:16. The conductor wrote my answer down in one line of the decisions file, under the researcher's pushback:

> "The outside is in balance: he keeps the claim." *(record)*

**Narrator.** I kept the claim. The line stayed in the talk, and the researcher's finding went on the record beside it. The book keeps both: my line, and the objection to it, and the fact that I read the objection and chose. Neither is withdrawn. If you think I was wrong, the researcher's sources are in the register, and you can check.

**Narrator.** This is the part of the afternoon I would most want a team to copy, and it is not my part. It is the storyteller's. A member found that the person it worked for had a weak line, and its job was to make sure he saw the strongest case against it, in writing, before the slides were cut. Not to win. To make sure the decision was a decision.
