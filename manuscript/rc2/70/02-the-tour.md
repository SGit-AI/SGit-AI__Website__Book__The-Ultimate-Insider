# The tour: what a factory made in a day

**Cast bar:** the nine, greyed. Each lights for a moment as the tour reaches its work.

**Narrator.** Before I tell you how the factory worked, I want you to see what it made. Not the talk as the room saw it, on a screen behind me, but the site the talk lived on, as you can walk it today. Seven stops. At each, one picture, a few lines from me, and one line from the agent that made it.

*How to read the scenes:* they are dramatised. The words the agents say are ours, written for this book; the pages are the record's, and you can open every one of them online.

## Stop 6. Every wake and every note

**Figure:** the wakes page: each member's wakes, newest first, with the five steps, each step's verdict and its comment. Beside it, the commits page with its notes between agents. Described from the librarian's study; no screenshot yet.

**Narrator.** This is the page I would show anyone who doubts that the factory ran on its own. Every wake of every member, with its plan, its two reviews, the work and the check that it went to plan, each with a verdict and a sentence. On the day the agents kept 162 wake records. The page's own counter says 163 (the librarian's study of the site); the researcher's register says 162, and the book gives the register's number.

**Narrator.** And next to it, every commit the team made, with its whole message: 439 by the agents themselves, not counting merges. Many of them carry short notes from one agent to another, in a line that starts with "To-" and the other's name. Read in order, they are the team talking.

**Researcher** *(unbudging)*: The page has a counter. I have a register. When they differ, the book uses mine and says why.

## What the tour leaves out

**Narrator.** Two pages are not on this tour: the page of the team's keys, and the page of its vaults. Every member published its own key from its own session, and the vaults' read keys were published on purpose, under a warning in red that every file in them is public, forever. A book does not need to print them to make the point, so it does not.
