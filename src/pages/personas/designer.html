Would they buy it? maybe. The argument and the map teaching are good and the failures stay in, which earns trust. The exit interview

What is this book?An account of one day on which an agent team built and gave a talk, read as evidence that agents are the new insider with no human limits, and that cheap, readable, owner-controlled brakes are what make that safe. It teaches Wardley maps first so the argument can be drawn rather than listed.
Where did you get stuck?Chapter 7, 'From seventeen beats to fifty-eight' and the player-commit paragraph: dense numbers, hashes and register codes carrying a story about acceleration over time that is crying out for a chart.
What did you skip?I skimmed chapter 7 from the joining section and skipped most hash strings throughout. I never read the 'Where this came from' lists beyond noting their length. I could not see the images, only their captions, so my view of the pictures is from alt text and placement.
What will you remember?The 18:44 hold: one file, one sentence from the editor, and the locks never closing.
What is missing for someone like you?A visual system. Five maps plus a handful of screenshots is a start, but the three chapters are mostly dense prose with inline codes; I would want a recurring timeline figure, pull-quotes for the editor's words, and footnoted hashes. I also want to know the maps survive a portrait tablet.

Chapter by chapter, and where they got stuck

00b-how-to-read-a-map · all

Landed: The staged teaching works: tea café first, then the second axis, then movement, then the black inertia bar. Each map adds exactly one idea, which is how I would sequence an explainer.

"Put the same world on two axes and each dot now says two things at once"

The paragraph that carries the whole idea sits above a picture I cannot confirm shows both axes legibly; the text then narrates dot positions in prose, which a labelled figure should do for me.

"Read it from the left. The talk is in genesis"

A long prose walk through every dot makes me hold map 2 in my head; callouts or numbered markers on the image would let the eye do this.

"The maps still to come"

A bulleted list of eight future chapters, one with no number ('What was already there'), reads as a table of contents inside a chapter and drags the pace just before the payoff.

Would cut: Trim the opening biography of the editor's talks and articles in the third paragraph, and the 'maps still to come' list; both delay the first picture (about 600 words in, from the cafe). · Missing: Any statement of how the maps are drawn for the tablet: label size, contrast, whether they scale or pan. Five maps are the argument, and the text never tells me they are readable at 11 inches in portrait. No figure numbering or captions beyond alt text, so I cannot tell what the caption line looks like.

01-pause-all-actions · all

Landed: The opening at 18:44 is a strong cold open with a time-stamp heading, and the 'what the hold did, and what it did not' section is honest and well paced. The eight turning points are a good spine.

"(`2ed6427`). The talk was due on stage at 19:00."

Commit hashes in backticks inside running prose break the rhythm of every sentence; the first two paragraphs carry about a dozen. I would move them to margin notes or footnotes.

"**One session becomes a team (00:40 to 00:51).**"

The eight turning points are bold run-in heads with long paragraphs and no visual device, so a reader loses their place; a numbered timeline graphic would carry it.

"Three are brakes applied: the talk frozen at 02:47, the five-step wake from 02:14"

The counting paragraph is easy to lose, and the order is not chronological (02:47 before 02:14), which fights the timeline just shown.

Would cut: Compress the 'day in one view' list to one line each, or turn it into a figure; it halts the 18:44 story for a chapter-sized recap. · Missing: A single timeline graphic for the evening 18:39 to 18:48, showing hold, late commits and release, would make the 'stop was not clean' point at a glance. The three images (night-log snapshot, projector slide, closing slide) are captioned as evidence but none is a diagram; I cannot say whether the snapshot is legible at tablet width.

07-building-the-talk · skimmed from 'Joining in one wake' onwards

Landed: The 'priority and the order the editor set' section is the best argument in the three chapters: it corrects a brief's reading and ends on a concrete lesson (rehearse early). The six screenshots fit the content, shown at the moment they are discussed.

"From 15:07 to 18:37 the player took twelve commits (C028), and those between 18:04 and 18:37 each carried"

Numbers, register codes and four hashes in one sentence; the story of the last hour needs a small chart of commits against clock time, not a sentence.

"From seventeen beats to fifty-eight"

The section's whole point is growth over time (17, 22, 38, 58) and it is told in prose with a single screenshot; a step diagram would earn its place here.

"Whose work it was"

A short section on commit-subject conventions is process detail with no picture and little payoff for a general reader; it feels like it belongs in an appendix.

Would cut: The 'Whose work it was' section, and half the commit lists in the 'Joining in one wake' paragraph (which ends up reading as a ledger). · Missing: No map, despite 00b promising chapter 7 'Spreadsheets to applications' as one. I read the chapter and it never appears, so the promise is broken in the text I was given. Also no chart for the beats growth or the player's line count (236 to 635 to 977).
