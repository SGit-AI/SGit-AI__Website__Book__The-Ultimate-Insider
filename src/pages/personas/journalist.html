The exit interview

What is this book?A forensic account of one day, 29 October 2026, in which a team of AI agents ran a talk's preparation and was stopped by its owner's usage limit before its own safety lock was applied. It argues that agents with no human limits are 'the ultimate insider', and that controls the owner can pull are what make that survivable.
Where did you get stuck?The first page of chapter 1 and the commit-hash density throughout: I cannot tell which of fifteen hashes matter, none is a link, and the key claims point to register IDs (C006, C027) I cannot open from the page. For a reporter that is the 'source beside the claim' problem exactly.
What did you skip?The turning-points tour in chapter 1, the countdown and arithmetic in chapter 8, and the count-reconciliation bullets in chapter 11. I also skimmed every 'Where this came from' list; they are thorough but I would not check them by hand.
What will you remember?The lock tool was built, tested and ready, and was never run; the brake that fired was the human's usage limit, not any review.
What is missing for someone like you?A two-sentence plain statement of the finding up front. Clickable links from claims to commits. Some outside voice that is not the book's own team or a sibling agent team. Any stated limit on what a single-account, single-model-family experiment can show. The impact: what was lost on stage?

Their own questions

What is the headline, and does the book earn it?Headline: 'AI agent team halted by its owner's usage limit 16 minutes before its own safety lock was due to close; the lock never did.' The book earns the facts (hold at 18:44, no lock ever run) but the 95% rests on the editor's word, and the 'brakes let you go faster' framing is the author's thesis, not a finding.
What could you not verify from the record?The 95% usage figure (the book says no meter exists); why the lock was not run; whether the talk was affected; the Cold-Read team's work (a sibling repository I cannot open); and every C-number, since the register is not on the page.
Which failure is the most interesting, and is it told fairly?The lock that waited on one busy session while a fallback instruction replaced a mechanism. It is told fairly and plainly, though the chapter then argues the hold was the right winner, which tilts it. Second is the editor acting without the controls noticing, in chapter 11.

Chapter by chapter, and where they got stuck

01-pause-all-actions · all

Landed: The opening scene is a proper news lede: a timestamp, a one-line file, an editor saying 'pause all actions'. The admission that the stop was not clean, with three commits landing after the hold, made me trust the author more.

"The 95% is not a reading from any meter in the record."

Good honesty, but it means the chapter's anchor number is hearsay, and my headline now rests on one sentence the editor said.

"Register: C005, C006, C021, C024, C027, C037."

Claims are sourced to register IDs I cannot open from the page, so I cannot check the number beside the claim.

"A brake that has never been pulled is a brake nobody knows works. This one worked the first time."

Quotable, but 'worked the first time' sits oddly beside the later admission that the stop let work land and the lock failed.

Would cut: The eight-turning-points tour near the end, which is a table of contents in prose and a stack of commit hashes I would skip. · Missing: A plain-language sentence on what these agents actually were and what the talk was, early, for a reader who has not seen it. Hashes are cited but not linked, so I cannot click through to the record.

08-the-locks-that-never-closed · skimmed from 'The brake loosened in the same commit'

Landed: 'The failures kept' is the best section in the three chapters: four named failures, plainly stated, including the unstaffed security role and the lock that waited on one busy session. This is where the story is.

"The record does not say why the lock was not run, and this book does not claim to know."

Correct, but it is the central 'why' of the chapter and the reader is left with a gap exactly where the story should bite.

"The hold was the right one to win, and it is worth saying why rather than leaving it as luck."

The book argues the outcome was right, which reads as defending the team rather than reporting it; a sceptic wants that judgement left to the reader.

"We put brakes in cars so that we can go faster"

The thesis is borrowed from a talk I have not seen and cannot verify from the chapter. (Quote corrected by synthetic@ to the chapter's exact words; the reader had paraphrased it.)

Would cut: The countdown paragraph with its ten hashes and the 'Ninety minutes, three parts' arithmetic (74.9 against 90 minutes), which does not serve the story. · Missing: A one-line summary at the top of what actually went wrong and what it cost, in plain terms. Whether the talk suffered for the unlocked parts: the chapter defers to chapter 10, which I was not given.

11-what-the-cold-reader-saw · all

Landed: This is the most interesting chapter for me: a second team given only the repository, its findings set against the author's own, and the disagreements in counts laid out openly. The expired 'not one hold' line is a genuinely good detail.

"Nothing in the platform kept one team from seeing the other"

A huge claim for a security reporter: the blind test was not blind. It is buried in the last section and rests on one debrief.

"Both teams ran on the same account"

If both 'independent' readings ran on one account and one model family, the 'agreement' proves less than the chapter claims, and this is not weighed.

"Whether the line was spoken, cut or changed on the night"

I want the answer; the chapter says the transcript lacks it, but the claim is cited to a commit, not shown.

Would cut: The 'Where the two records disagree' bullet list of counts (106 vs 99 vs 105 commits), which is real but dull; keep one example. · Missing: Who the 'Cold-Read journalist' is: an agent whose pitches the book cites as corroboration. I need to know it is a model, and that agreeing with the author is not independent confirmation. No link to that repository.
