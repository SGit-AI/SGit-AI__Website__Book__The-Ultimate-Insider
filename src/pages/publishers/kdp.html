Everything for the author to do it by hand: the two print-ready files, what to put in each of KDP's three screens, the price, and how to order a printed proof before anything goes on sale.

The steps, in order (about 30 minutes; where this page and KDP's screen disagree, the screen wins)

  1. KDP Bookshelf: Create, Paperback.
  2. Paperback details: the texts in section 2.

  4. Order a printed proof (section 4): it comes marked "Not for resale", and nothing is on sale yet.
  5. Paperback rights and pricing: section 3. Publish only once the proof is in your hands and looks right.

Description

For anyone with an idea and no team yet.

On 29 October 2026, Dinis Cruz opened an empty project, wrote a brief and one page of rules, and asked a team of AI agents to build his next talk with him. Nine agents joined, each with one job written down. That evening, nineteen hours after the first commit, he gave the talk.

This book follows that day as a factory. Who did what, and when. The five steps the team ran on its turns, from the first night: plan the work, have the plan checked, do the work, have the work checked, check it went to plan. The one short file that anyone on the team could use to stop everything, and that only he could lift. And what did not work, because that is in the book too.

Where the agents speak, the words are written for the book; the events are the record's. Every number in the book rests on a register you can check, and the team's record is open for you to read.

Then it is your turn. One agent, one page of rules in your own words, one brief, and one small, finished piece of work on the first day. A second agent only when the first is busy. Alone or with others, you can start the same day.

Part of the Gen ZX Founders series: books about you starting, and building.

3. Rights and pricing

TerritoriesAll territories (worldwide rights: it is your own book).
Primary marketplaceAmazon.com (or Amazon.co.uk if you prefer the pound as the base).
List price$15 on Amazon.com (KDP2). KDP fills in the other marketplaces from it; round them by hand if you like round prices there too.
What it earnsAt 134 pages, large trim, standard colour, KDP's printing cost read on 1 November ($1.00 + $0.0402 a page) is about $6.39 a copy, so $15 earns about $2.61 at the 60% royalty; KDP's lowest allowed price would be about $10.65. KDP's own screen shows the exact figures for today.
Expanded distributionLeave off for now; it raises the lowest allowed price.

4. The printed proof, and author copies

  • A proof before publishing. Once the files are approved in the previewer, KDP offers Request printed proofs (on the Paperback content screen, and from the Bookshelf's "..." menu). You pay printing and delivery; proofs carry a "Not for resale" band. Order from Amazon.co.uk to have it delivered in London.
  • Author copies (no band, at printing cost) come only after the paperback is published: Bookshelf, "...", Order author copies.
  • The proof is the one to read with a pen, as you did with the A4 copy: the print review takes the next round of notes the same way.

Check before you upload

The front cover's small line still reads "RC2 · Nov 2026 · CC BY 4.0", as on the review copies. For the copy on sale you may want "First edition · November 2026" instead; say so and the cover is redrawn the same day, at the same size.
