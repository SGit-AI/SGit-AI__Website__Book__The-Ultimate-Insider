The story before the slides

Clock: Hour fifteen.

Narrator. The first thing the storyteller did was the thing I had asked for and not done myself: it put the story before the slides. The tree and the maps, as they were used

Narrator. The tree put the talk's beats in an order with a reason behind each one: what the room must believe first, and what follows from it. It is the shape of an argument, drawn before anyone drew a slide for it.

Figure: the storyteller's story tree as it was that afternoon: the talk's beats as branches, as its own text has them, with no turn marked yet.

Narrator. The maps did something different. They put the parts of my argument where they sit relative to each other: what depends on what, and how settled each piece is. The slide that carried one said, in small type, "positions, not measurements". The line is the honest heart of a map like that: it shows you where things are, not how big they are.

The objection kept

Narrator. My story for the talk leaned on a claim: that the outside of an organisation, the part facing the internet, had become smaller and more mature than it was. The claim compared the outside with itself, then and now.

Something to try

The storyteller was useful because it argued with the plan before anything was built. Asking one of your agents to argue against your plan, in writing, costs very little. You still decide; it just helps to have decided with the argument in front of you.
