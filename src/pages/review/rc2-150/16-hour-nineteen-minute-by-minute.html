The team racing its own locks

Clock: Hour nineteen.

Narrator. Part four is where the factory stops. In its last hour, the team was doing what it had done all afternoon, only faster.

Conductor: Part one is final, and nothing misses it: I flagged it ready for its lock. Deck put the title slide in front of it. The storyteller checked parts two and three and found a piece the editor had asked for that had landed in no beat. Deck planned its last session around parts two and three. And I flagged part one ready for its lock a second time.

Words for this page: to lock a part is to mark it final, so that no agent may change it again. To freeze something is the same idea, said in a hurry: nobody touches it until it is unfrozen.

Narrator. Meanwhile I was in the player, asking for fixes, and getting each one back in minutes.

The hold

Narrator. A quarter of an hour before the talk, I told the conductor to pause everything. What I said in the moment was that we were at 95% of my usage. The real reason was time. We had run out of it: there was nothing left to do that would make much difference without adding risk to what was finished, and I had got everything I wanted to do. That is the point of diminishing returns, and it is the moment to stop. The 95% was a coincidence. I would have gone to 100 if it had been needed.

Narrator. The conductor wrote one file. Here it is, as it was written:

"# HOLD: the editor's usage limit is at 95%; pause all actions"

>

"What lifts it: the editor deletes this file and re-enables the paused tasks. No member lifts it."

Narrator. The 95% is in the file because it is what I said in the moment. While that file exists, every member's next session stops before it starts. It says who wrote it, what I said, the state of each part, and what lifts it. And one line names the only person who can.

Conductor: I could keep going. That is exactly why I am stopping.

Narrator. What was already in flight still landed. A minute or so after the hold, the designer finished checking part two on a projector, found nothing to change, and said so. Then the day's last release went out.

Designer: The second part on a projector: ready. Nothing to change. Ah. The hold.

The locks overtaken from outside

Narrator. One more thing about this hour. The team had built its own brakes, the locks and the hold any member could pull, and its own locks lost the race. The thing that actually stopped the factory came from outside it: me, deciding that the day's work was done.

Something to try

A stop file stops the next session, not the one already running. If you keep one, it may help to know how you would see what is still running when you pull it, and how long you would wait for it to finish.
