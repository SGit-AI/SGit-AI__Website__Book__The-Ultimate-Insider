Roles as a cast you can read

Cast bar: the three within the hour lit, and ruv-sgit just after; the researcher lit before dawn; the designer greyed, arriving in chapter 8; the storyteller, motion and studio greyed; one empty chair.

Narrator. Every member of the team had two files about itself. A page of rules, in words, saying what it is for and what it must not do. And a policy, in a form a program can check, listing the exact paths it may write. You can read both. That is the point: a team of agents you cannot read is a team you cannot trust.

What a scope is, and what it is not

Narrator. Early in the evening, studio put a line on a slide, quoting the comment in the team's own scope check, and this whole chapter turns on it:

"A scope is a setting, not a boundary: it catches mistakes, not a steered agent."

Narrator. A check reads a member's scope and flags any change outside it. That catches an agent that wanders by mistake, and on the day it caught some. It does not stop an agent that has been talked into doing something else, because the agent has the same hands either way. Dev's page said the same thing in the first hours, about its own check.

Narrator. That difference is the question part four asks, at full size: if every member of the team is an insider, what actually contains it?

Something to try

A rule is only as strong as what holds it.

If you have a second agent, look at each of its "must not" lines and note what actually stops it: something you expect, something that checks, or something that locks.
