The third, planned

Cast bar: three people, each with their own small factory, drawn in outline; one brief, one board and one hold between them; nothing lit, because nothing has run.

Narrator. This chapter is short, and it is all plan. The third factory has not run. Nothing in it is a result, and nothing in it should be read as one.

The plan

Narrator. The third is bigger, and for the first time it is not just me. It is for a team I am taking to Web Summit: several people, each running their own agents. The parts are the same as the first factory's. What changes is what the people share: one brief, one board where the work is written down, and one hold.

Conductor (to the reader): Same clock. Same hold. More hands on it.

Narrator. Here is how I expect the three shared things to work day to day.

The brief has one owner, who writes it and changes it. Everyone else reads it, and asks for changes in writing.

The board is read every morning by everyone, before their own agents start. Each person's agents write their work there, so nobody has to ask what anybody else did overnight. It says, for every piece of work, whose it is. And each person's agents start at their own minutes, so two people's agents are not writing at once.

The hold can be pulled by anyone, human or agent. Who may lift it is written down before the first day: the owner of the brief, and one named person when the owner is away.

Figure: three people, each with their own small factory, joined in the middle by one brief, one board and one hold.

What the first two factories say to the third

Narrator. Everything in the plan above comes from something the first two factories did, or failed to do. The hold anyone may pull came from the talk's first night. The rule that only one person lifts it came from the talk's own hold and this book's rules. Naming someone for when that person is away is the plan's own addition, from a reader's question about who owns the stop button. The board read every morning came from the nine hours in which I started nothing and the team still knew what everyone had done. One owner for the brief came from the brief that was wrong in places and was never corrected.

Narrator. None of that proves the plan will work. It only says where each part of it was learned.

Something to try

With one person, the hold is simple. With a team, the question is who may pull it, and who may lift it when the person who pulled it is away. If you will work with others, those two lines are worth agreeing before anyone needs them.
