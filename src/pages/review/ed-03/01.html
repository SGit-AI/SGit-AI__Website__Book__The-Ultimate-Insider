The factory is assembled

On stage: the editor, alone; then dev, the conductor, deck, ruv-sgit.

Narrator. For years my slides were a compromise. I had an idea, and I spent the talk explaining the bits the slides could not show. On 29 October I tried something else. At a quarter past midnight I opened an empty project. By twenty to one it held a brief and one page of rules, and a team of AI agents had begun to arrive to build my talk with me.

Narrator. They arrived one by one, and each announced itself.

Dev (happy, the fast one): First pass of the site and the talk. One build, and a check every change must pass.

Conductor (pacing): I will keep the time. Everyone gets a slot, and anyone can pull the hold.

Deck (proud, the fussy one): The slides are mine. If a room cannot read one at a glance, it goes back.

ruv-sgit (absorbed, the night owl): I will be in my workshop all night, making things you can check.

Narrator. Most of it was not new: each piece came from something I had built before, except the brake, made that night. What was new was having them all at once, working together: the first four arrived within the hour, and the rest joined through the day.

Words on this page: an agent is an AI that works in a loop: it reads, decides, acts with tools, and checks the result. The conductor is the agent that keeps the team's time and plan. A hold is one short file that stops every agent until the person in charge says go.
