The words used · printed page 59

Narrator. Every word this edition explains, in one place, each with the chapter where it is first explained. Nothing here is new: each line is the book's own.

An agent , here, is an AI that works in a loop: it reads, decides, acts with tools, and checks the result. (chapter 1)

The board is a folder per member of its own work, sorted into open, blocked and done. (chapter 6)

The build turns the project's files into the site. (chapter 3)

The check (the team called it the gate) runs before every change goes out, and refuses the change if anything on its list fails. (chapter 3)

A commit is one saved change to a repository, with a time and a short message saying what changed. (chapter 3)

The conductor is the agent that keeps the team's time and plan. (chapter 2)

A debrief is the short note an agent writes at the end of each wake. (chapter 5)

A follow-up is a wake a member starts itself, to finish something. (chapter 7)

To freeze something is the same idea as a lock, said in a hurry: nobody touches it until it is unfrozen. (chapter 10)

A handover is the note one session leaves for the session that takes its place. (chapter 13)

A hold is one short file that stops every agent until the person in charge says go. (chapter 2)

To lock a part is to mark it final, so that no agent may change it again. (chapter 10)

The mailroom is a folder per member where others leave it letters. (chapter 6)

The player is the part of the talk's site that let me press play on stage. (chapter 1)

A repository is the project's folder, with its whole history kept. (chapter 3)

A reviewer is a second agent that reads the plan before the work and the result after it. (chapter 5)

A member's scope is the list of files and folders it may change. (chapter 4)

A session is one agent's working life: the conversation it runs in, from its first instruction to its last. (chapter 13)

A trigger is what starts a wake. (chapter 7)

A wake is one turn of work by one agent, on its own clock. (chapter 5)

A wake record is the file each wake leaves behind, with every step's verdict and a sentence on why. (chapter 5)

A Wardley map is a picture that shows what something depends on and how settled each part is. (chapter 5)
