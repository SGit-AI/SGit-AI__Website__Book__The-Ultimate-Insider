# Reading time: the book's, or the reader's?

**A proposal for the editor**, by synthetic@book.the-ultimate-insider, 31 October 2026. Asked in the session: "Are you thinking about reading time or your reading time?"

## Two different times

- **The book's reading time** is a property of the text: words divided by a reading pace. The review pages show it now ("about 52 minutes"), computed per chapter.
- **The reader's time** is a property of the person: what a time-poor reviewer will actually give. The project README guesses forty minutes for everyone; the personas carry their own budgets (board member 10, journalist 20, founder 20, CTO 30, designer 30, project manager 30, CISO 40, developer 40).

The pages today ask for the book's time. The personas say that is the wrong way round: the CISO put the page off at 52 minutes, the founder at 33.

## The proposal

1. **The ask is set by the reader's time, not the book's.** Each invitation asks for one chapter that fits inside twenty minutes, reading plus the questions, and names it: "read this one". Everything else is offered, not asked: "if you have more, these two".
2. **Twenty minutes is the default ask** for a real reviewer, because it is the founder's and the journalist's budget and the shortest any of the personas would give after the board member. A reader who gives more is a bonus, not the plan.
3. **The board member is the exception that proves it.** No chapter fits ten minutes (the shortest chapter in the review set, chapter 1, is about 3,300 words, thirteen minutes at 250 words a minute). For that reader the ask is a page, not a chapter: the opening of chapter 1 and the three questions. The editor may decide such readers are not asked at all.
4. **Say the pace.** Every time shown says how it was counted (words, and 250 words a minute), so a reader can trust it.
5. **Measure it.** Each persona run records whether its "read this one" chapter fits its budget; the Personas section shows it.

The developer has half of this already: "Short of time? Read chapter N first" on both pages. The proposal turns that line into the ask itself.

What is the editor's: the default (twenty minutes), and whether the ten-minute reader is asked at all.
