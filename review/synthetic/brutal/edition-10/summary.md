# The 10-page edition, read brutally (A11)

**SYNTHETIC READERS: test instruments, never readers. A read of a draft.** By synthetic@book.the-ultimate-insider, 2 November 2026, for the editor's A11 (T11: "Every passage is tested by a reader who asks: why do I need this, do I have the context, why is it here, does it make sense to me now"). Three fictional readers, chosen to be hard to please (a student, the Gen X founder, the journalist), went passage by passage through the storyteller's revised 10-page edition (`chapter-plan/cast/edition-10.md` at `ea67f6f`). Each passage got keep, cut, move, explain first or shorten, with one sentence why. Reader by reader in this folder.

## The count

About 60 passages each. Most pass: the student and the journalist kept about half, the Gen X founder about three in five. Matched across the three readers (78 passages placed in the draft; twelve openings could not be placed exactly because of markup and were left out of the count), **18 passages fail for two or three of the readers.**

## What fails, by kind

1. **Insider moments with no setup** (ten of the eighteen). The scenes assume the reader knows what was at stake: who ruv-sgit is and what it makes, the researcher's withdrawn objection ("which file, what rule, who decided?"), the designer's "my table", the "@" and "the count", "does not hold" with no claim stated, studio's "relayed word", the lock that was never turned, "parts two and three", "the split". Each needs one clause of setup, or a cut. The fix is small: the moment is good, the reader just arrives without the fact it turns on.
2. **The logistics the editor named** (all three). "Zoom in: each beat is a chapter in the thirty-page edition..." is a pointer to a book the reader does not have: cut it, or say in a line what the longer edition adds.
3. **Page 2, the series page** (all three would cut it if they could cut one page). It is the only page without a scene, and it arrives "before I have a reason to care". The Gen X founder: "fold one plain sentence into page 1". The series framing is the editor's open decision (G1); the readers say only where it costs them.
4. **Notes in the wrong place.** "How to read the scenes: they are dramatised" on page 1 comes before any scene (two of three: move it to just before the first scene). The "Words on this page" box comes after the words it explains (the student) or is too heavy at five terms on a phone (the journalist).
5. **Claims a sceptic flags.** Web Summit on page 9 arrives without context and reads to the journalist as a plug; the closing of page 6 ("neither of us had to pretend") is "sentiment, not method" (the Gen X founder) and "the editor's reading, not the record" (the journalist).

## What passes best

All three name the same scene: "At 18:44, sixteen minutes before the talk was due," the hold. Then the conductor's "Caught by the reviewer I invented.", and "Start with one of me". The book's best page is still the moment it stops.

## For the storyteller, in order

1. One clause of setup for each insider moment on pages 3, 5, 6 and 8, or cut it (the ten are listed reader by reader in the JSON files).
2. Cut "Zoom in ... thirty-page edition", or replace it with one line on what the longer edition adds.
3. Move "they are dramatised" to just before the first scene; put each gloss at its first use.
4. Page 2: the editor's call (G1); if it stays, one plain sentence of why this is for the reader, and the series line after the first scene.
5. Web Summit: one line of context, said as plans, not results.
