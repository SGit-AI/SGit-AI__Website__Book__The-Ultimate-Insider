## Section by section

- **Holds for all three:** "The empty project", "The clock and the five steps" ("I could copy it" and "I could run them tomorrow"), "Before dawn", "The argument with a reviewer" (the best scene for all three), "The objection kept", both "Do this now"s.
- **Stalls all three: "The map, before its name"** (chapter 2). A Wardley map described in words with no picture; for the first-build finisher "where I felt the book might be for programmers".
- **Stalls two: "Four arrivals"** (four agents and their moods at once) and **"Sent back, and owned"** (four incidents with different pairings; "I lost who sent what to whom"). All three would cut or trim the "@" episode.
- **Stalls the first-build finisher only:** "Caught by its own reviewer", "The empty chair", "The creator comes back" and "A relayed word refused", for unexplained words (repository, commit, debrief, ported) and timestamps.

Best lines: "I am not asking for an exception; I am asking you to read the line again." (the newsroom builder and the first-build finisher); "It looks slow written down. The checking is what let nobody watch." (the conference team member).

## For the writer, in order

1. "The map, before its name": the picture on the page, or the section to two or three sentences.
2. "Sent back, and owned": two incidents, not four; the "@" episode cut or given one line on why the name counts.
3. The storyteller and motion: one thing each does on the page that no other agent does.
4. "Four arrivals": one arrival to a line, so the other three stand out.
5. For a first-time builder: repository, commit and debrief glossed where first used, and fewer timestamps in "The creator comes back".
