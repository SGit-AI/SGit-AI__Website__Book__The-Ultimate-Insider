- **What holds:** the problem told plainly ("I talked over my own pictures", the best line for two; "The idea was a movement. The slide was a still. I was the bit in between." for the third), the loop ("see it, ask for it differently, see it again", a pattern all three have used or can), and a do-this-now small enough for tonight.
- **Where all three stall: "Pressing play".** The long quoted passage about users, agents and gold describes an animation they cannot see, before they know why it matters; to the first-build finisher "it was for programmers". All three: cut it to one plain sentence of what the animation showed.
- **Where all three stall: "Inventing on principle".** A named talk from 2012 and a concession that the day did not live up to it: "pleasant", but it slows the chapter. All three: two lines, or the end notes.
- **"What a factory is" comes late and as a list** for two of three: "the build, the check every change had to pass, the brief, the brake" are words without pictures, and the cast is "only promised, never shown" (a row of greyed cards). One line per agent's job, before the do-this-now, is what would make them want to meet the cast.
- **The conference team member's do-this-now** is about one idea, not their team; one line on writing the sentence together, as a team, would close it.

## For the writer, in order

1. "Pressing play": the gold passage to one plain sentence of what the animation showed.
2. "Inventing on principle": two lines, or to the notes.
3. "What a factory is": one line per agent's job, so the cast is shown before it is promised; the list of four nouns with a picture each, or fewer.
4. "Do this now": one line for a team writing the sentence together.

## Limits

Three readers, one session, a first draft. The scores are prompts for the writer; real readers, chosen by the editor, are the test that counts.
