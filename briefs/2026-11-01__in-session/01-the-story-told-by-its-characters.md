## How the conductor reads it (not his words)

- **INK1, signed:** standard colour (the publisher took it at his word in its own session, commit 461f925). **The developer:** its task re-enabled with him (commit e6cdc7f).
- **NR1, signed as the direction, tried first on the opening pages:** the book is told by its cast. He is a persona too, and his voice is the default narrator; the agents come in turn and tell the story from their side: what each saw, the problem it hit, what worked and why (the prompts, the anchored data, the growing context). Very visual: each agent's mood on the page (happy, worried, stressed, unhappy). Creative forms: one agent interviewing another, a therapy session, an agent complaining. Each agent a distinct persona (clothes, hair, almost a culture, a little stereotypical, like the icons), introduced with a scorecard in the manner of Top Trumps cards; the reader always knows which agents exist at that moment. Narrative first: the informational material (hashes, ids) leaves the page for the online reader's augmented mode.
- **What it does not change:** NS1 (the name and structure) stays open; the experiment is the opening pages, the ten-page sample and a couple more.
- **"a tightness"** is read as a transcription of something like "a teaching point" or "attentiveness"; the point stands either way: the reader should see what each agent is doing and how it is coping.

## The conductor's view, given to him (his question: "What do you think?")

It is the strongest idea for the book so far: it turns a record into a story people follow, and the agents explaining their own work (why the anchored data keeps them honest, why a long context loses things) teaches the method better than any diagram. Four things keep it true:

1. **Dramatised, and marked as such.** Moods, interviews and therapy sessions are scenes the team writes; each rests on something in the record (what the agent did, got wrong or wrote), and the book says plainly that the scenes are dramatised. The historian grounds every scene.
2. **What an agent says about how it works must be true.** "Hard to hallucinate because the data is anchored", "the context gets big, so things are lost": the researcher checks each such line against sources, so a reader can rely on it.
3. **The failures stay in.** An agent complaining, or stressed, is where the honest parts live: the skipped step, the claims that did not hold, the hold itself.
4. **Guard rails as before:** characters of AI agents; nothing attack-shaped; no real person but the editor, who asked to be in it; the data and hashes kept, but in the augmented mode, not on the page.
