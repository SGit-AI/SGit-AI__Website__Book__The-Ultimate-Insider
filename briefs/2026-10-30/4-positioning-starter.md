# Positioning starter: for the market stream to test, not to adopt

## Readers (hypotheses)

1. **The security leader** who has been told agents are coming and has no model of the risk. Buys the argument; keeps the book for the controls.
2. **The engineering lead** who has tried agents, got little, and suspects the problem is the setup, not the model. Buys the method; the record convinces them.
3. **The founder building with agents** who wants to run a team like this on Monday. Buys the playbook.
4. **The GRC and audit reader** who needs to say what "controlled" means for an agent. Buys the control register and the signed decisions.
5. **The Web Summit crowd**: curious, short on time, wants the one-day story and the number 816.

The question for each: what do they call the problem, in their words; what would they pay; where do they put the book down.

## The one sentence (candidates)

- One person, ten agents, one day: how a talk got built, and how the team that built it was kept in check.
- Agents are the ultimate insider. Here is the record of a team of them, and the brakes that let it go fast.
- The counter-example to "agents don't work": nineteen hours, 816 commits, on stage, with every decision signed.
